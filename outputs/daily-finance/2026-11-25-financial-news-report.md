## Key Financial News
- The US Trade Representative's office proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners — including China, Japan, the EU and South Korea — after an eight-month Section 301 probe into unfair trade practices tied to excess factory capacity; China faces a proposed 20% tariff on top of existing duties from Trump's first term. The proposals are not yet in force and remain subject to public comment, with final action possible as soon as January.
- The tariff news combined with a hotter-than-expected October core PCE inflation print (3.3% y/y vs 3.2% expected; headline PCE 3.8%, roughly as expected) to spark an early tech-led selloff: the Nasdaq fell as much as ~4.5% at the open and the S&P 500 as much as ~3%, with chipmakers hit hardest — Nvidia fell ~8.7% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped as much as ~7% intraday.
- Markets pared most of the losses by mid-to-late afternoon, with the Dow turning positive (last ~+0.6%) while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq trimmed their declines to roughly -0.1% and -0.6% respectively as of the intraday snapshot above — a volatile, headline-driven session rather than a clear trend reversal.
- Traders priced in roughly a 98% probability of a further Fed rate hike at the December meeting following the hot PCE data, extending the hiking cycle that has weighed on rate-sensitive and high-multiple tech names through November.
- The ASX 200 (confirmed close) fell 0.39% (-34.0 points) to 8,692.4, giving back some of Tuesday's rebound, after Australian October CPI data showed headline inflation accelerating to 4.9% y/y (from 4.3%) — the highest since 2023 — with the RBA's preferred trimmed-mean measure rising to 3.8% from 3.6%, both above forecasts.
- Within the ASX, defensives such as healthcare, telcos and insurers were bought (ResMed +1.5%) while gold miners, tech and lithium names sold off sharply (Elevra Lithium -9.5%, Liontown falling heavily), reflecting a rotation away from rate-cut-sensitive and higher-beta sectors.
- Gold fell roughly 0.9% to about $3,524/oz and Brent crude slipped about 1.2% to ~$87.68/bbl (WTI testing the $80 level) as hawkish Fed repricing lifted the US dollar; AUD/USD reversed earlier gains to trade about 0.5% lower after the hot domestic CPI print reduced RBA easing expectations.

## Investment Implications
- Wednesday's sharp intraday reversal — from a ~4%+ Nasdaq slide at the open to a much milder decline (and a positive Dow) by the afternoon — suggests markets are still digesting conflicting signals (tariff risk, hot inflation, AI-capex doubts) rather than settling on a clear direction; confirmation of the official close will matter for gauging whether buyers or sellers ultimately won the session.
- The newly proposed tariffs on 14 trading partners, especially the elevated China rate, add a fresh source of trade-policy uncertainty on top of already-hawkish Fed pricing (~98% odds of a December hike), a combination that has repeatedly pressured high-multiple tech and semiconductor names through November.
- In Australia, the hotter-than-expected October CPI print further diminishes the odds of near-term RBA rate cuts and reinforces a "higher for longer" domestic rate backdrop, which is weighing on gold miners and growth/tech stocks while favouring rate-insensitive defensives.
