# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-09-16

## Executive Summary
- The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25bp to 3.75%-4.00% — its first hike since 2023, voted 12-0 — with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh flagging persistent inflation risk and 16 of 18 officials pencilling in another hike later in 2026.
- US equities fell after the decision (S&P 500 -0.45% to 7,551.81, Dow -1.21%/-631.21pts to 51,461.90, Nasdaq Composite roughly flat -0.01%), while the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.008%, its first time above 5% since July 2007.
- The ASX 200 rose 0.28% to 8,696.5 during Wednesday's Sydney session (ahead of the US Fed announcement), led by gains in Energy, Resources and Gold stocks.

## Key Financial News
- **Fed hikes for the first time since 2023**: The FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate 25bp to a target range of 3.75%-4.00%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh highlighted persistent inflation risk in his press conference, and the updated dot plot showed 16 of 18 policymakers expecting at least one more hike before year-end. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html))
- **US equities sell off post-decision, Dow hit hardest**: The Dow Jones fell 631.21 points (-1.21%) to 51,461.90, its worst day in weeks, as rate-sensitive and cyclical names led losses. The S&P 500 slipped 0.45% to 7,551.81 while the Nasdaq Composite was roughly flat, down 0.01% to 25,978.42. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-september-16-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-fed-meeting-decision-080356525.html), [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-sept-16-2026))
- **10-year Treasury yield breaks above 5% for first time since 2007**: The benchmark yield climbed to about 5.008% following the hike and Warsh's inflation comments, a level last seen in July 2007, pressuring equity valuations and raising borrowing-cost concerns. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/treasury-yield-bond-market-fed-decision.html))
- **ASX 200 rises ahead of the Fed announcement**: Sydney-session trading (which concludes before the Fed's decision, due to time-zone timing) saw the ASX 200 add 0.28% to 8,696.5, with Energy, Resources and Gold sectors leading gains. ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/australia-stocks-higher-at-close-of-trade-spasx-200-up-028-4902939), [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-16/asx-markets-business-live-news/107157842))
- **Gold slips on the hike despite earlier haven bid**: Gold traded around $4,310/oz following the Fed's decision, edging down from an earlier intraday level near $4,275-4,338/oz reported through the session, as the hike and hawkish tone weighed on the metal. ([Kitco News](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-09-16/gold-price-drops-4310oz-fed-votes-12-0-favor-25-bps-rate-hike-16-18))
- **Oil mixed, still elevated on Middle East risk**: Sources showed a wide spread — WTI reported variously around $102-105/bbl and Brent around $107.72-108.34/bbl — reflecting continued volatility tied to Saudi pipeline and regional supply disruptions rather than a single clean print. ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/crude-oil))
- **Iron ore futures edge up, no confirmed fresh spot print**: China's DCE most-traded iron ore contract rose to 709 yuan/tonne (+0.14%), its first gain after six straight declines, though spot cargo prices reportedly eased further; no confirmed USD/tonne benchmark print for September 16 was found, so the prior confirmed range (~$95-96/T, September 15) is carried forward for reference only. ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104118243-data-shfe-dce-market-movement-sep-16))

## Investment Implications
- The first Fed hike since 2023, combined with a 10-year yield above 5% for the first time since 2007, signals a materially tighter rate environment; portfolios with heavy exposure to long-duration growth equities or rate-sensitive sectors may see continued volatility.
- The Dow's outsized decline relative to the S&P 500 and Nasdaq suggests cyclical and value-tilted names bore the brunt of the post-Fed selloff, while mega-cap tech was comparatively resilient — a divergence worth monitoring into the next session.
- Australian assets face a cross-current: local equities rose Wednesday on sector-specific strength, but confirmation of the Fed's hawkish stance (after the ASX close) and the RBA's own September 29 decision leave AUD and rate-sensitive ASX sectors exposed to reassessment.
