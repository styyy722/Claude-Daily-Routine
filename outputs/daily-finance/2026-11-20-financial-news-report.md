## Key Financial News
- US equities fell sharply through Friday's session, extending Thursday's six-session losing streak; the sell-off began in Asia, where Taiwan's Taiex dropped 6.2% and the Nikkei and Kospi also fell sharply, and spread through Europe into US futures and the cash session.
- Chip and AI-infrastructure names led losses on renewed doubts that heavy AI data-centre spending will generate the profits investors expect; AMD fell roughly 8% on a new short-seller report alleging its firmware reached a blacklisted Chinese server maker, while Nvidia was reported subpoenaed by California over China chip-export concerns, extending its slide to roughly 22% over nine sessions.
- Nvidia's leadership uncertainty continued following Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO, with reports that Nvidia had approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a potential successor while interim CEO Colette Kress continues to run the company.
- The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) jumped roughly 20% intraday to above 34 — firmly in "high fear" territory — up from Thursday's close of 28.44 (itself up nearly 16%).
- Rate markets priced a 93.9%–96.4% probability (CME FedWatch) of a quarter-point Fed rate increase at the December 9 meeting, even as the 10-year Treasury yield fell intraday to around 4.92%, consistent with safe-haven buying during the equity sell-off.
- UnitedHealth fell a further ~5% on proposed Medicare Advantage audit rules and Democratic proposals to expand Medicare, adding to sector-specific pressure already seen earlier in the week.
- The ASX 200 closed at a 100-day low, down 1.58% (-137.8 points) to 8,592.6, with the index down 5.39% for the week and materials stocks down 12.3% for the week as none of the 11 sectors finished higher; the session also reflected disappointment among Australian investors who were largely under-allocated in the Anthropic IPO (CommSec reportedly filled around 14% of bids, other brokers as little as 7%, with Australia's total allocation of roughly 1.32 million shares — about US$110 million — around a third of its earlier SpaceX allocation).

## Investment Implications
- The breadth and speed of the sell-off (VIX up ~20% intraday, S&P 500 on track for its worst week since April 2025) suggests markets are actively repricing the near-term payoff timeline for AI infrastructure spending rather than reacting to a single data point.
- Falling Treasury yields alongside still-hawkish Fed rate expectations point to safe-haven flows into bonds amid the equity rout, a combination worth watching for signs of a broader shift in rate expectations once Friday's close and next week's data are confirmed.
- Gold's sharp intraday decline alongside a broad equity sell-off is an unusual combination worth noting rather than a clear directional signal; it may reflect liquidation to cover losses elsewhere rather than a change in the metal's longer-term safe-haven demand.
