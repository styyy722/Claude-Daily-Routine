# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-09-30

## Executive Summary
- US equities closed September mixed-to-lower even as August PCE inflation cooled more than expected (headline 3.4% y/y vs. 3.7% forecast), with the S&P 500 -0.25% to 7,651.54 and Dow -0.86% (-443.87pts) to 50,906.05, while the Nasdaq Composite bucked the trend +0.24% to 26,861.06; both the S&P and Dow posted monthly losses for September.

- The ASX 200 extended its post-RBA-hike gains, +0.92% to 8,789.30, while AUD/USD ticked up modestly to 0.6995; a US government shutdown risk tied to the Sept 30 fiscal year-end was already defused after Congress passed a continuing resolution funding the government through Dec 11.

## Key Financial News
- **August PCE inflation cools more than expected**: The Fed's preferred inflation gauge rose 3.4% y/y (vs. 3.7% forecast) with core PCE at 3.0% y/y and +0.3% month-on-month, prompting traders to pull back October rate-hike odds to ~35% from ~51% the prior day per CME FedWatch, though a December hike is still broadly expected. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/pce-inflation-eases-to-34--cooling-the-case-for-another-fed-rate-hike-125513195.html), [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/traders-pull-back-on-october-fed-hike-bets-after-cool-pce-data))
- **Wall Street closes out a losing September**: Despite the softer inflation print, the S&P 500 (-0.25%) and Dow (-0.86%) both fell on the month's final session and posted monthly losses for September, while the Nasdaq Composite (+0.24%) was the lone major index to finish green. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/stock-market-today-live-updates.html))
- **Treasury yields hit fresh multi-decade highs**: The 10-year yield pushed above 5.30% intraday — described by some outlets as its highest level in roughly 24 years — while the 30-year rose to ~5.64%, continuing to pressure equity valuations and gold even as the inflation data cooled. ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/ng/news/2026/09/30/us-10-year-treasury-yield-hits-24-year-high-above-530percent/))
- **ASX 200 extends post-RBA gains**: The index rose 0.92% to 8,789.30, building on strength following the RBA's 25bp hike to 4.60% a day earlier, even as US peers struggled. ([Market Index](https://www.marketindex.com.au/news/asx-200-live-today-wednesday-30th-september))
- **US government shutdown risk already defused**: With Sept 30 marking the US fiscal year-end, a funding cliff had loomed, but Congress passed a continuing resolution in early September (signed Sept 2) funding the government through Dec 11, removing an overhang that could otherwise have weighed on sentiment into quarter-end. ([Wikipedia – 2026 US federal government shutdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_federal_government_shutdowns))
- **AUD/USD ticks higher but remains soft on the month**: The pair rose 0.13% to 0.6995, though it remains down about 2.4% over the past month even as it holds a 5.8% year-on-year gain. ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/australia/currency))
- **Gold and oil under mixed pressure**: Gold slipped 0.37% to $4,166.60/oz (down over 6% for the month) as high yields continued to weigh on bullion, while Brent added 0.97% to $97.09/bbl; WTI figures varied by source and are not reported as a single precise change. ([Trading Economics – Gold](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold), [Trading Economics – Brent](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil))

## Investment Implications
- The cooler PCE print reduces near-term Fed tightening risk, but long-dated Treasury yields still pushing to multi-decade highs suggests markets are pricing pressures beyond near-term Fed policy (e.g. term premium, fiscal supply), which may keep valuation multiples under pressure regardless of the softer inflation data.
- The ASX 200's continued strength against a softer Wall Street session points to local factors (post-RBA positioning, sector rotation) rather than a synchronized global risk-on move; investors should watch whether this divergence holds once global cues reassert into October.
- With the US shutdown risk removed, one tail risk is off the table for now, but elevated real yields continue to weigh on rate-sensitive assets like gold, arguing for continued caution on long-duration and non-yielding exposures until yields stabilise.
