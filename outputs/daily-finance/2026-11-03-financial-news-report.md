## Key Financial News
- The RBA's Monetary Policy Board raised the cash rate target by 25bp to 4.85%, a 6–3 majority decision; the three dissenters favoured holding at 4.60%. The Board judged "a further tightening in financial conditions is warranted" given inflation expected to stay above 3% over the coming year and tighter domestic capacity than previously estimated.
- US equities closed mixed: Nasdaq Composite +0.83% to 25,372.01 and S&P 500 +0.36% to 7,568.92, lifted by a chip-stock rally, while the Dow Jones fell 0.38% to 51,786.72 as Apple — a heavily-weighted Dow component — dropped on weak margin guidance.
- Apple beat fiscal Q4 EPS estimates ($2.10 vs. $1.99 expected) but guided gross margin to 45–46%, citing a global memory-chip shortage, and reported weaker-than-expected Q4 iPad sales; shares fell.
- Palantir beat Q3 estimates and raised full-year guidance, but shares slipped on a sharp slowdown in new contract bookings and valuation concerns.
- A reported US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island lifted oil prices and the 10-year Treasury yield (to ~4.95%) overnight, with Iran saying it is ready to respond "forcefully"; WTI crude ended the US session little changed at $96.58/bbl (+0.12%) after an earlier premarket spike.
- Gold fell 0.96% (-$38.00) to $3,931.60/oz as elevated Treasury yields weighed on bullion.
- Chevron reported Q3 adjusted EPS of $5.28, beating the $4.83 consensus estimate.
- US midterm election voting began Tuesday, with results due overnight AEDT; analysts flagged uncertainty for the dollar, Treasury yields, and policy-sensitive sectors heading into the result.

## Investment Implications
- The RBA's fifth hike of 2026 takes the cash rate to a level last seen in 2008, raising the cost of borrowing further; rate-sensitive ASX sectors (banks, REITs, retailers, housing-linked names) and mortgage holders are likely to feel continued pressure, while a hawkish RBA stance could support the AUD.
- The divergence between Apple's margin-driven selloff and Palantir's bookings-driven pullback suggests investors are increasingly scrutinising AI-era profitability and sustainable growth rather than rewarding beats alone — a theme likely to persist through the earnings season.
- Rising Treasury yields on Middle East risk premia add a second headwind to high-multiple tech and AI-infrastructure names already under valuation scrutiny; energy-sector positioning should account for continued headline sensitivity to Iran-related developments.
