## Key Financial News
- ASX 200 closed up 1.1% at 9,129.0 (intraday high 9,159.8), its best close since 25 August; all sectors bar utilities rose, led by real estate (+2.2%, rebounding after Monday's slide) and industrials (+2.3%). Qantas rose ~5.6% after announcing 850 head-office job cuts and an outsourcing deal with Accenture; Monadelphous jumped ~14% on first-half revenue guidance of roughly A$1.7bn; Elsight gained double digits on a ~US$1m defence order. Life360 fell 13.5% on its quarterly results, and Woolworths slipped nearly 1% after closing at a record $42.47 the prior session.
- Wall Street's Monday close was its strongest single day since early April: S&P 500 +2.41% to 7,554.67, Dow +2.21% (+1,131.81 pts) to 52,417.54, Nasdaq Composite +2.67% to 25,235.49, led by chipmakers rebounding from the prior week's AI-regulation-driven correction. Nvidia was the exception, falling ~5-6% to around US$177 after reports of a board investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's reported ties to a suspected Chinese-intelligence contact; shareholders have since filed a proposed class action over the allegations.
- Oil remained the dominant macro driver: Brent fell more than 5% Monday to ~$92.57/bbl, capping a 16% weekly slide — its steepest since the pandemic began — after President Trump said fresh strikes on Iran were highly unlikely while negotiations continue; Tuesday's Sydney-session reading had Brent easing a further ~1.1% to ~$91.56/bbl. Iran has said it will not negotiate without US concessions, and shipping incidents near the Strait of Hormuz plus an attack on a Saudi Aramco office in Riyadh show regional risk is far from resolved.
- Markets are pricing an ~85% probability of a further Fed rate move at the December meeting following stronger-than-expected US jobs data; US October CPI is due for release after Tuesday's ASX close (headline forecast +0.3% m/m, core +0.2% m/m), making it the key catalyst for Wednesday's Sydney session.
- China reported a US$1.04 trillion trade surplus for the first ten months of 2026, with October exports up 35.2% year-on-year, underscoring continued export strength even amid global trade tensions.
- NEXTDC paused data-centre projects that lack signed customer contracts following a delay to its Melbourne build; Goodman Group and Digital Realty each hold a 9.9% stake in the company. Separately, Anthropic is reportedly seeking to raise up to US$69.3bn in an IPO that could rank among the largest on record.
- AUD/USD was broadly flat around 69.2 US cents, with gold easing ~0.5% to roughly $4,118/oz as risk appetite firmed and safe-haven demand eased; iron ore ticked up ~0.1% to around $93.05/t in midday Sydney trade.

## Investment Implications
- Monday's Wall Street rebound and oil's continued slide both point to the AI-regulation selloff easing for now, but Nvidia's governance overhang (board probe, class action) is a reminder that concentrated key-person and geopolitical-compliance risk in mega-cap AI names has not gone away.
- The oil collapse — driven by de-escalation signals on Iran rather than a demand shock — is disinflationary for now, but shipping incidents near Hormuz and the Aramco office attack mean the risk premium could snap back quickly if talks stall.
- A broad-based, ex-utilities ASX rally led by industrials and real estate (rather than banks or miners alone) suggests domestic risk appetite is improving on the back of the US rebound; Wednesday's US CPI print is the next major swing factor for both markets.
