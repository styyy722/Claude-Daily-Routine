# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-09-07

## Executive Summary
- US markets (NYSE, Nasdaq) were closed Monday for the Labor Day holiday; US equity levels below carry forward Friday 4 September's close.

## Key Financial News
- **US markets closed for Labor Day**: NYSE and Nasdaq were shut Monday 7 September, with no official price movement in the S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite or Dow Jones; markets reopen Tuesday 8 September, when they must digest last week's strong payrolls print and the weekend's US-Iran escalation in one move. ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/09/61646501/is-stock-market-open-today-labor-day-2026-september-7))
- **ASX 200 ends flat as energy offsets tech weakness**: The index closed +0.06% at 9,011, with strength in non-energy minerals, energy and logistics (Woodside +1.6%, Santos +1.7%) offset by weakness in technology, commercial services and healthcare (Xero -3.9%, WiseTech -3.8%); US equity futures dipped on renewed Strait of Hormuz strikes affecting regional shipping. ([InstaForex](https://www.instaforex.com/forex-news/3145419))
- **Ingenia Communities surges on rejected takeover bid**: Ingenia Communities Group jumped nearly 15% after rejecting a takeover approach from Warburg Pincus, the ASX's standout mover of the session. ([InstaForex](https://www.instaforex.com/forex-news/3145419))
- **Oil holds war-premium gains**: WTI traded near $92.53/bbl, holding above $90 after closing Friday at $91.48 — up roughly 9.7% for the week — as traders continue to price Strait of Hormuz supply-disruption risk from the ongoing US-Iran tanker conflict into front-month crude. ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/oil-markets-latam-monday-september-7-2026/))
- **Iron ore tops $100/ton for first time in seven weeks**: Singapore futures rose as much as 1.6% to $101.10/ton, the highest intraday level since 2 July, as traders unwound bets favouring coking coal and positioned for expected pre-holiday restocking demand from China. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/iron-ore-tops-100-on-position-unwinds-and-china-buying-hopes))
- **Gold retreats on Fed rate-hike repricing**: Gold fell toward $4,400/oz, extending Friday's ~2% decline to around $4,380, as a stronger US dollar and growing expectations of an imminent Fed rate hike — following Friday's much-stronger-than-expected August payrolls (+162K vs ~53K expected) — weighed on the metal despite ongoing Middle East safe-haven demand. ([LiteFinance](https://www.litefinance.org/blog/analysts-opinions/short-term-analysis-for-oil-gold-and-eurusd-for-07092026/))
- **Australian labour market resilient; China data awaited**: Australian job advertisements rose for a second consecutive month in August, supported by seasonal Christmas hiring; investors are also positioning for key Chinese economic data this week, including August CPI, PPI and trade figures, which will feed through to iron ore and AUD sentiment. ([InstaForex](https://www.instaforex.com/forex-news/3145419))

## Investment Implications
- With US markets shut Monday, Tuesday's reopen carries elevated event risk: it must simultaneously price in Friday's strong payrolls beat (pushing Fed hike odds higher) and the weekend's direct US-Iran naval exchange in the Strait of Hormuz.
- The ASX's rotation into energy and resources and away from technology reflects investors positioning for a sustained commodity-driven inflation impulse from Hormuz-related oil risk, even as gold — typically a geopolitical hedge — is being pressured by rate-hike expectations instead.
- Iron ore's break above $100/ton adds a incremental tailwind for ASX miners and the AUD heading into this week's Chinese CPI, PPI and trade data, which will be a key swing factor for both.
