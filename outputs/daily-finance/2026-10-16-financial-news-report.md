## Key Financial News
- **Tech-led selloff drags Nasdaq down over 2%**: The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.14% to 26,140.03, its steepest one-day drop in weeks and capping its worst week since late June (down roughly 4.5% for the week), as AI-trade anxiety intensified; the S&P 500 lost 1.09% to 7,659.44 while the Dow's heavier weighting toward financials and industrials limited its decline to 0.34% (52,002.41).
- **Chip and software stocks bear the brunt**: Palantir fell about 7% and software stocks collectively shed an estimated $260 billion in market value; memory-chip names remained under pressure on continuing reports of advances by China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), reviving competitive concerns first flagged earlier in the week.
- **AI-infrastructure debt concerns persist**: CoreWeave shares fell again amid ongoing scrutiny of its debt-heavy balance sheet, extending a theme that has weighed on AI-adjacent names through the week, even as some analysts (reportedly including Bank of America) characterised the broader AI trade as "resilient."
- **Inflation and rate-hike odds swing intraday**: Hot inflation data earlier in the week had pushed up bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike at the October 27–28 FOMC meeting, though those odds reportedly eased later in Friday's session; the Fed's next scheduled decision remains October 28.
- **Volatility spikes as yields hold near multi-year highs**: The VIX jumped over 21% to just under 20, while the US 10-year Treasury yield held little changed near 5.08–5.10%, a level participants have flagged as historically elevated this cycle.
- **Oil rallies on Middle East supply risk**: WTI crude rose about 2.6% to ~$99.01/bbl (with Brent reportedly touching $110/bbl) amid reports of escalating Iranian activity around the Strait of Hormuz; gold added a more modest 0.36% to ~$4,185/oz.
- **ASX 200 and AUD/USD firmer in local trade**: The ASX 200 rose 1.00% to 8,806.70 and AUD/USD gained 0.36% to ~0.6943 during Friday's Sydney session, which closed before Wall Street's later tech-driven slide; no independently verifiable sector-level breakdown for this specific session was located.

## Investment Implications
- The widening gap between the Nasdaq's sharp weekly decline and the Dow's relative resilience signals a continued rotation away from richly valued AI/chip names and into financials, energy and other cyclicals — a trend worth tracking into next week's trading.
- Persistent scrutiny of AI-infrastructure financing (CoreWeave's debt load, the CXMT competitive threat to memory-chip makers) suggests investor conviction in the AI capex cycle is being tested, which could continue to pressure capital-intensive AI and data-center-linked equities.
- Simultaneous strength in oil, a VIX spike, and still-elevated bond yields point to a market pricing both geopolitical risk and renewed inflation/rate uncertainty — a combination that has historically coincided with choppier, more defensive equity positioning.
