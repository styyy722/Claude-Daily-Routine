# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-10-05

## Executive Summary
- US equities extended Friday's rally into the new week: the Nasdaq Composite closed at a fresh record on continued AI/tech-sector strength, while the S&P 500 and Dow also finished higher.

## Key Financial News
- **Nasdaq hits a fresh record close**: The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.05% to 27,477.31, extending the AI/tech-led rally that has powered the index through much of 2026, as investors returned to mega-cap technology names.
- **S&P 500 and Dow also advance**: The S&P 500 gained 0.66% to 7,773.95 and the Dow Jones added 0.18% to 51,267.90, both building on Friday's gains.
- **Weak jobs report continues to shape Fed expectations**: Friday's September nonfarm payrolls report (+29,000 versus roughly 79,000–90,000 expected, unemployment up to 4.2%) remains the dominant driver of rate expectations heading toward the 27–28 October FOMC meeting; the 10-year Treasury yield rose modestly to 5.31% (+3bps) on the day.
- **Oil falls as OPEC+ decision is digested**: WTI crude dropped 2.02% to $89.28/bbl after Sunday's OPEC+ agreement among its seven core members to hold November 2026 production at September's required levels was absorbed by markets as a "no change" outcome rather than a bullish supply signal; the next OPEC+ meeting is 1 November.
- **ASX 200 little changed**: Australia's benchmark edged up just 0.05% to 8,686.40, continuing to digest the RBA's 29 September rate hike to 4.60%; the next RBA decision is not due until 2–3 November.
- **AUD/USD firms modestly**: The Australian dollar traded around 0.6973 against the US dollar, broadly stable to slightly firmer as markets continue to price US and Australian rate paths.
- **Gold edges higher**: Gold futures rose 0.15% to $4,168.90/oz, a modest gain as safe-haven demand and rate expectations remained in balance.

## Investment Implications
- The Nasdaq's record close underscores that AI/tech-sector strength remains the dominant driver of US equity returns; investors with concentrated technology exposure should be mindful of how reliant current index gains are on a narrow set of mega-cap names.
- Oil's decline on the "no surprise" OPEC+ outcome suggests the market had already priced in continuity; near-term crude direction is likely to be driven more by demand-side data and global growth signals than further OPEC+ headlines before the 1 November meeting.
- With the RBA on hold until 2–3 November and the Fed's next decision not until 27–28 October, near-term AUD/USD and ASX 200 direction is likely to be shaped more by incoming US economic data than by domestic policy news in the immediate term.
