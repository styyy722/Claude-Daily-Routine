## Key Financial News
- Nvidia confirmed Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO following reports of two alleged extramarital affairs, one involving a woman reportedly scrutinised by US intelligence over a possible link to a suspected Chinese-intelligence-connected individual; CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO and director Harvey Jones was named the company's first board chair, with Huang (who holds about 3.61% of the company) departing the board. Shares plunged as much as 14% at Friday's open before closing down roughly 5%, with retail investors buying the dip heavily; analyst Dan Ives cut his price target to US$225 from US$300 while keeping an outperform rating.
- Wall Street closed lower on Thursday (S&P 500 -0.35% to 7,592.40, Dow -0.36% to 52,297.09, Nasdaq Composite -0.39% to 25,366.09) as the Nvidia headlines weighed on sentiment, even as Broadcom rose about 5.5% and energy stocks gained on higher oil prices; markets are now fully pricing in at least a 25bp Fed rate increase in December after hot US inflation data, with Friday's producer price report also in focus.
- The ASX 200 fell 64.6 points (-0.7%) to 9,082.0, now 2.3% below its 52-week high, on broadly negative breadth (44 advancers, 147 decliners). Materials led losses (-1.7%) despite firmer gold, with Financials (-0.7%) and Information Technology (-0.6%) also weak; Utilities (+1.5%), Telecommunications (+0.2%) and Consumer Discretionary (+0.1%) were the only sectors to close higher. Among stocks, Paladin Energy dropped 16.9%, Lendlease fell about 6-7% after launching a A$1.22 billion bond redemption, while Xero (+2.1%), ResMed (+1.8%) and Computershare (+1.7%) outperformed.
- Bond yields spiked sharply, which Market Index cited as a key driver of the ASX sell-off: the Australian 10-year yield rose 9 basis points to 5.13% by 3pm AEDT, while the US 10-year yield was reported around 5.1% in Friday's pre-market update — both markedly higher than Thursday's levels.
- NAB CEO Andrew Irvine and CBA CEO Matt Comyn appeared before a House Economics Committee hearing chaired by Ed Husic. Irvine faced questions over roughly 2,500 job cuts and the shift of IT work to India and Vietnam, saying the changes were not AI-driven; NAB has repaid about A$170 million to underpaid staff and paid a A$2 million fine. Comyn was questioned on an estimated A$270 million in excess fees charged to low-income customers, of which CBA has repaid A$68 million; Husic said he intends to keep pursuing the remainder.
- AUD/USD slipped to around 0.6799 (-0.1%), close to a 10-month low, while gold rebounded about 1% to roughly US$3,914/oz after sliding to near US$3,852-3,876 on Thursday. Brent crude eased 0.2% to US$95.62/bbl but remains elevated after reports of attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and disruption risk in the Strait of Hormuz; President Trump said the US does not need a deal with Iran. Iron ore was little changed at roughly US$91.6/tonne.
- A global Microsoft outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for part of the session (services were gradually recovering, with no indication of a cyber attack), and reports emerged that Anthropic is seeking a valuation of up to US$1.4 trillion ahead of a potential IPO.

## Investment Implications
- Nvidia's leadership transition has resolved the acute governance overhang that weighed on the stock this week, but a near-14% intraday swing on the news shows AI-megacap volatility remains elevated and could still spill into broader technology sentiment depending on how the market judges Colette Kress's succession.
- A synchronised spike in US and Australian bond yields alongside broadly negative ASX breadth (147 decliners vs 44 advancers) suggests rate-sensitive sectors — property, growth names and even gold miners despite a firmer gold price — remain exposed if yields continue climbing into the Fed's December decision.
- Parliamentary scrutiny of NAB and CBA over job cuts/offshoring and excess fees adds a reputational and potential regulatory overhang for the major banks, even though Financials held up relatively better than Materials on the day.
