## Key Financial News
- ASX 200 closed up 0.10% at 9,029.7 (All Ordinaries +0.20% to 9,163.4) after an early slide to 8,968.5; 8 of 11 sectors finished lower, but Materials (+2.88%) and Energy (+0.73%) led gains as gold, critical-minerals and base-metals names rallied (BHP +2.74%, Rio Tinto +1.55%, Sandfire Resources +5.65%, Lynas Rare Earths +6.82%); James Hardie jumped 6.86%.
- ANZ fell 3.20% to $39.05 despite reporting a $7.3bn FY26 annual cash profit, dragging the broader banks lower alongside Commonwealth Bank (-0.92%); Financials (-1.11%) and Real Estate (-1.27%, Scentre -4.43%, GPT -3.47%) were the weakest sectors.
- US markets rebounded sharply Monday — intraday readings had the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq Composite each up roughly 2.4%–2.9% — as a slide in oil prices eased inflation fears and investors bought back into stocks after last week's correction-territory selloff tied to fears of tighter AI-infrastructure/data-centre regulation following the Democrats' midterm wins.
- Nvidia bucked the rebound, falling as much as 8% intraday (around -5% by mid-afternoon) after New York Times reporting that CEO Jensen Huang had a "close" relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed (a Singapore-based cloud firm and Nvidia customer) whom US intelligence suspects of passing information to Chinese intelligence; Nvidia has denied wrongdoing, describing the contacts as routine business interactions, while Senator Elizabeth Warren has pressed Nvidia's board over its export-control oversight.
- Oil extended its slide from last week, with WTI and Brent crude both down several percent through the session on hopes that falling prices reflect, and could further support, an easing of US-Iran tensions; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Sunday that Iran peace talks appear stalled, adding some uncertainty to that narrative.
- The VIX remained elevated (28.18 at Thursday's close) even as equities rallied, reflecting lingering caution after last week's AI-regulation-driven selloff; the US 10-year Treasury yield eased to roughly 4.84%, down from about 5.21% in mid-October, consistent with softer inflation expectations from falling oil.
- Investors are looking ahead to fresh US inflation data (CPI) and producer prices (PPI) later this week, along with earnings from Occidental Petroleum (Monday), CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials, for further direction after a volatile week.

## Investment Implications
- Monday's rebound looks driven largely by falling oil prices and bargain-hunting after a sharp correction rather than a resolution of the underlying AI-regulation overhang, so volatility in AI-adjacent and chip names is likely to persist until policy clarity improves.
- Nvidia's governance-related selloff is a reminder of concentrated "key-person" and geopolitical-compliance risk in mega-cap AI names; investors with heavy AI/semiconductor exposure should watch for further disclosure or congressional developments.
- The ASX's afternoon recovery, led by materials and gold rather than banks, suggests domestic investors are leaning into commodity and critical-minerals exposure for diversification while bank earnings (ANZ) disappoint.
