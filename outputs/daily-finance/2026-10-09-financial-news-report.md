# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-10-09

## Executive Summary
- US stocks rallied to cap a volatile, record-setting week: the S&P 500 (+0.59%), Nasdaq Composite (+0.64%) and Dow (+0.83%, up over 400 points) all rose as oil eased and AI/tech names rebounded after a Bloomberg report that OpenAI expects $70bn+ in annualized revenue by year-end, offsetting Thursday's weaker Financial Times estimate.

## Key Financial News
- **AI stocks rebound on OpenAI revenue update**: Thursday's selloff, triggered by a Financial Times report pegging OpenAI's annualized revenue near $50bn (below prior estimates), reversed Friday after Bloomberg reported OpenAI expects $70bn or more by year-end, lifting tech and semiconductor names.
- **Telecoms slide on SpaceX competition fears**: Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile each fell more than 7% after SpaceX's satellite-to-phone spectrum deal raised the prospect of new mobile competition; SpaceX-linked shares rose about 1.6% on the news.
- **Treasury yields stay elevated**: The 10-year yield held near 5.24%, not far from the 24-year high touched earlier in the week, keeping pressure on rate-sensitive sectors even as equities rallied.
- **Oil eases, Iran risk persists**: Brent crude slipped more than 1% to around $104/bbl and WTI was little changed near $91.56, after President Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November midterms; Trump also said Russia would begin supplying US and global diesel markets.
- **Fed independence concerns**: Trump formed a committee to investigate mortgage-fraud allegations against Fed Governor Lisa Cook and whether they justify her removal; Cook denies wrongdoing, and the Supreme Court ruled in June she may continue serving while the matter is unresolved.
- **Consumer sentiment weakens**: The University of Michigan's preliminary October reading fell to 46.3, its lowest since May, with households citing cost-of-living pressure from energy prices, the Iran conflict, and tariffs.
- **Company news**: Delta Air Lines missed Q3 estimates and cut guidance as fuel costs rose 62% y/y (~$6bn higher for the year); American Express was fined $350m over anti-money-laundering control failures; Moderna, Pfizer, BioNTech and Merck gained on a reported cancer-vaccine initiative.

## Investment Implications
- Friday's rebound in AI and semiconductor names suggests Thursday's selloff was driven by a single, now-revised data point rather than a broad reassessment of AI spending, but revenue disclosures from major AI players remain a swing factor for the sector.
- Treasury yields near a 24-year high are an ongoing headwind for valuations, particularly growth and rate-sensitive stocks; the upcoming 27-28 October FOMC meeting and bank earnings starting next week are key catalysts to watch.
- Headline risk around Iran, the Fed's independence, and softening consumer sentiment are each capable of resurfacing market volatility even as major indexes sit near record levels.
