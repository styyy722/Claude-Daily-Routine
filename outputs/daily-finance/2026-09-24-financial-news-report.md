# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-09-24

## Executive Summary
- US equities were roughly flat as hopes of a Strait of Hormuz de-escalation deal offset renewed bond-market pressure, with the S&P 500 essentially unchanged (-0.02% to 7,704.13), the Nasdaq Composite eking out a fresh record (+0.01% to 26,939.37), and the Dow slipping 0.31% to 51,349.98, even as the 10-year Treasury yield held near a 19-year high (~5.14%).
- President Trump and President Xi Jinping met at the White House; Treasury Secretary Bessent announced a two-month extension of the US-China trade truce (to January 10), though the summit was widely characterised as heavier on symbolism than substance, with little concrete progress on tariffs, AI, critical minerals or Taiwan.
- The ASX 200 fell 0.72% to 8,702 after Australian unemployment rose to a post-COVID high of 4.6% in August, with RBA rate-hike odds for the September 29 decision remaining very high (estimates ranging ~80-96.5%) despite the softer labour print; oil (WTI) jumped 2.94% on lingering Middle East supply risk even as diplomatic progress was reported.

## Key Financial News
- **US equities close roughly flat as Hormuz diplomacy offsets bond-yield pressure**: The S&P 500 finished essentially unchanged (-0.02% to 7,704.13) and the Nasdaq Composite notched a marginal record close (+0.01% to 26,939.37), while the Dow lagged, down 0.31% to 51,349.98; Reuters reported US and Iranian negotiators were working on a plan to end the Middle East conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which helped indices trim earlier, steeper losses. Philadelphia Fed President Paulson said "some modest further tightening may be warranted," reinforcing the hawkish tone. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/24/g-s1-144806/trump-xi-summit), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/24/politics/trump-xi-meeting-china-ai))

## Investment Implications
- The 10-year Treasury yield sitting near a 19-year high while equities stay roughly flat suggests markets are increasingly decoupling rate risk from near-term equity direction, but continued yield pressure (and now a 7.37% 30-year mortgage rate) raises the risk of a delayed drag on rate-sensitive sectors such as housing and small caps.
- The Trump-Xi trade-truce extension removes a near-term tail risk but is a short, two-month rollover rather than a durable resolution, so trade-policy headline risk is likely to resurface as the January 10 deadline approaches.
- Persistently high RBA rate-hike odds despite a weaker August jobs report indicate the central bank is prioritising above-target inflation over labour-market softening, which keeps a September 29 hike as the base case and argues for continued caution on rate-sensitive ASX sectors (property, retail).
