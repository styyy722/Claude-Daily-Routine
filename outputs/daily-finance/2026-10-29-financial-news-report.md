## Key Financial News
- **Alphabet tumbles despite cloud strength**: Alphabet (GOOGL) closed down 12.64% to $283.82 after third-quarter adjusted EPS of $2.67 missed the roughly $3.02 consensus, even as revenue of $127.6B (+25%) was only slightly below estimates and Google Cloud revenue of $30.7B more than doubled, beating expectations and aided by TPU system sales. The market focused on free cash flow swinging to a burn of roughly $9.1B after capex of ~$51.4B outran operating cash flow; Alphabet maintained its 2026 capex range of $195B–$205B and signaled a further step-up for 2027.
- **Microsoft shrugs off its own earnings**: Microsoft (MSFT) closed essentially flat (-0.16% to $530.14) despite beating on both revenue ($92.3B, +19% vs. ~$90.7B expected) and adjusted EPS ($4.89 vs. ~$4.73 expected). It disclosed an Azure dollar figure for the first time (~$33.2B, +48%), with total Microsoft Cloud revenue of $63.9B (+30%) and commercial remaining performance obligations up 73% to $677B. Free cash flow of ~$18.6B came in well above some estimates, and capex of ~$45.2B landed below management's own ~$50B quarterly sketch, which it attributed to delays; Microsoft guided to 49%–50% Azure growth next quarter.
- **Dispersion within the former "Magnificent Seven"**: Meta rebounded 1.96% to $624.52, stabilizing after last week's roughly 12% post-earnings slide tied to a legal charge and AI-spending concerns. Amazon fell 4.25% to $243.79 ahead of its own Q3 report due after Thursday's close, with the market seemingly de-risking into results. The split reactions underscore that hyperscaler earnings are now being judged individually on capital discipline and free cash flow rather than moving the group together.
- **Index-level divergence**: The Nasdaq Composite's 1.38% decline was disproportionately driven by Alphabet's steep single-stock drop, while the S&P 500 (-0.30%) and Dow (-0.25%) posted far milder declines — suggesting this looks more like an Alphabet-specific reset than a broad unwind of the AI trade.
- **Treasury yields ease post-Fed**: The US 10-year yield slipped to roughly 4.94% (-0.08pp) as markets continued digesting Wednesday's Fed decision to hold rates at 3.75%–4.00% with a historic joint governor/regional-president dissent.
- **Hot Australian CPI ahead of RBA**: The ABS's September 2026 monthly CPI indicator, released October 28, showed annual inflation accelerating to 4.3% (from 4.0% in August) with trimmed mean (underlying) inflation at 3.6% (up from a revised 3.5%). The hotter print adds to the case some economists have already made for a hike at the RBA's November 3 decision, with the cash rate currently at 4.60% after September's increase.
- **AUD and gold both softer on the day**: AUD/USD eased 0.36% to ~0.7029 even against the hot CPI print, while gold fell 1.14% to ~$4,021/oz; neither move was tied to a specific identifiable catalyst beyond a generally softer commodities/risk tone alongside the Nasdaq's tech-led pullback.

## Investment Implications
- Alphabet's plunge despite strong cloud growth, contrasted with Microsoft's muted reaction to a cleaner beat, suggests investors are now differentiating hyperscalers by capital discipline and free cash flow rather than rewarding AI-driven revenue growth alone — a shift worth watching into Amazon's results.
- The hot September CPI print (4.3% headline, 3.6% trimmed mean) strengthens the case for an RBA hike on November 3; AUD/USD's modest dip today despite the print may reflect broader risk-off flows from the Nasdaq selloff rather than a reassessment of RBA odds.
- The narrow, single-stock-driven nature of today's Nasdaq decline (S&P 500 and Dow both far milder) argues against reading this as a systemic AI-trade unwind, but Amazon's Thursday-night earnings are the next real test of that view.
