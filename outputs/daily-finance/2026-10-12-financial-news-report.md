## Key Financial News
- **US equities mixed on a holiday-thinned session**: The Dow added 195.60 points (+0.38%) to 51,850.55 as investors rotated into value names, while the S&P 500 (-0.06% to 7,807.24) and Nasdaq Composite (-0.44% to 27,245.90) slipped; Micron Technology fell 3.9%, the single biggest drag on the Nasdaq.
- **Columbus Day holiday**: US banks and the bond (Treasury cash) market were closed Monday, leaving equities to trade on lighter volume ahead of a busy data and earnings week.
- **Iran de-escalation headline eased some risk**: President Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November midterm elections, helping calm some of the geopolitical risk premium that had been building in markets.
- **Telecom infrastructure rally in the US, but telco selloff in Australia**: US tower/satellite-connectivity names Crown Castle (+15.6%) and American Tower (+15.5%) surged on optimism tied to satellite-to-phone infrastructure deals, even as the same SpaceX spectrum-deal story continued to pressure Australian telcos (Telstra -2.5%, TPG -4.9%) and US carriers last week.
- **ASX 200 rallies on banks and miners**: The index rose 0.76% to 8,783.2 (All Ordinaries +0.67% to 8,937.0), with Financials (+1.8%) and Materials (+1.3-1.8%) leading; BHP rose 2.5% to A$62.49 on firmer commodity prices, while a 4.3% overnight jump in a US-listed copper miners ETF lifted Sandfire Resources (+3.4%) and other miners.
- **Weak US consumer sentiment**: The preliminary University of Michigan October consumer sentiment reading fell to 46.3 from 48.1, extending a run of soft confidence data even as equities held up.
- **RBA rate-hike speculation continues**: The RBA's cash rate sits at a 15-year high of 4.60% after its late-September hike, and it has signalled it "could tighten again if inflation remains stubborn"; investors are now weighing the odds of a further hike at the RBA's November meeting.

## Investment Implications
- The divergence between a holiday-thinned, mixed US session and a strongly rallying ASX 200 suggests local commodity and bank strength is currently doing more to drive Australian market direction than the overnight US lead.
- With Q3 US earnings season starting this week (major banks first) and CPI due Wednesday, volatility is likely to pick up from Monday's quiet, low-volume holiday session as markets reprice Fed rate expectations.
- The US-listed tower/infrastructure rally contrasting with the Australian telco selloff highlights that the same spectrum-competition story is being read very differently by two separate markets; investors with exposure to both US and ASX telecom/infrastructure names should note the diverging reaction.
