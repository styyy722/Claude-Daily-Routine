## Key Financial News

- US equities closed a volatile week mixed: the S&P 500 added 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.39% to 26,522.55, while the Dow shed 95.40 points (-0.18%) to 51,682.64, as traders digested this week's first Fed rate hike since 2023. ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-sept-18-2026))
- The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed back above 5% to ~5.004% (+~6bps), after touching 5.041% mid-week — its highest level since 2007 — as investors weighed the Fed's signal that further tightening remains possible. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/treasury-yields-fed-rates-volatile-week.html))
- The ASX 200 finished flat at 8,731, with strength in tech, industrial services and non-energy minerals offset by weakness in healthcare and energy names after RBA Governor Michele Bullock struck a hawkish tone in parliamentary testimony, warning inflation remains stubbornly high with upside risks building. ([Market Index](https://www.marketindex.com.au/news/asx-200-live-today-friday-18th-september))
- Oil prices eased as Saudi Arabia's damaged East-West pipeline continued its restoration, with WTI trading $99.39-101.64/bbl and Brent $98.46-104.33/bbl, both retreating from the week's highs on reduced Middle East supply-disruption fears. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/price-of-oil-09-18-2026/))
- Gold opened near $4,381.60/oz (-0.4% from Thursday's close) before rebounding intraday to touch a weekly high of $4,439.80/oz as inflation concerns eased following the Fed's decision. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-price-today-friday-september-18-2026-gold-hits-weekly-high-as-inflation-concerns-fade-104807567.html))
- AUD/USD traded around 0.7141, within its recent weekly range of roughly 0.7086-0.7176, as the RBA's hawkish rhetoric provided some support to the currency.
- No fresh USD/tonne iron-ore spot print was confirmed for September 18; the last confirmed level (~$97.42/T, September 17) is referenced only, not treated as a new figure. ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/iron-ore))

## Investment Implications

- The Fed's hawkish first-hike signal and Treasury yields holding above 5% point to continued volatility for rate-sensitive and growth sectors in the near term.
- RBA Governor Bullock's hawkish parliamentary testimony raises the odds of a "higher for longer" domestic rate path, a potential headwind for Australian rate-sensitive sectors (REITs, discretionary retail) heading into the September 29 decision.
- Continued restoration of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and easing oil prices could relieve some global inflation pressure if the trend holds, a mild positive for risk assets.
