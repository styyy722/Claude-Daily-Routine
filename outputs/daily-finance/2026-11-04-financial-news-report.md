## Key Financial News
- Democrats won control of both the US House and Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections; markets read the result as reducing political uncertainty, with the VIX volatility index sinking roughly 20% and the Dow on pace for its biggest one-day gain since April 2025.
- US equities rallied sharply through Wednesday's session: the Dow was up ~2.9% (~+1,511 points) and the S&P 500 ~+1.8% as of a 3pm ET update, with the Nasdaq Composite ~+1.9% as of a 2pm ET update; Apple shares hit a record high, while chip names including Nvidia and Micron were mixed to lower and Trump Media & Technology shares fell nearly 17%.
- Oil prices plunged roughly 8% (WTI to ~$88.08/bbl, Brent to ~$94.66/bbl, per Oilprice) after Vice President JD Vance linked Tuesday's Republican midterm losses to the Iran war, with traders pricing in a swifter resolution to the conflict.
- The US 10-year Treasury yield eased to around 4.90% (sources range 4.898%–4.92%), down from ~4.95% on Tuesday, as the election outcome and falling oil prices weighed on yields.
- The ASX 200 fell 0.59% to 8,834.5 in Wednesday's Sydney session — its second straight weak session — as fears that a Democrat-controlled Congress could bring greater scrutiny of AI investment intensified a sell-off in mining stocks; energy, utilities and banks outperformed the broader market.
- AUD/USD traded near 0.6965 in Wednesday's Asian session, holding just above a two-week low, as the US dollar was weighed down by midterm-related uncertainty (single-sourced, per FXStreet).
- Iron ore (Singapore 62% Fe) ticked up to around $91.9/t from $90.7/t on Wednesday, per SP Angel's market note (single-sourced).

## Investment Implications
- Reduced political uncertainty from a clear midterm result removed one overhang for US equities, but the durability of Wednesday's rally will likely depend on corporate earnings and Fed policy rather than the election outcome itself.
- Sharply lower oil prices on hopes of Iran de-escalation could ease global input-cost and inflation pressures — a tailwind for consumer-facing sectors, but a headwind for energy producers and resource-heavy indices such as the ASX 200.
- The divergence between a buoyant Wall Street and a mining-led ASX pullback shows domestic-specific drivers — the RBA's rate path and AI-regulation risk under a Democrat-controlled Congress — are shaping the Australian market somewhat independently of the US election relief rally.
