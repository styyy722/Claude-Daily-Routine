## Key Financial News
- US equities staged a partial rebound on Friday: the Nasdaq Composite climbed 1.09% to 24,579.42 and the S&P 500 added 0.31% to 7,376.86, led by chipmakers Micron and Nvidia, while the Dow lagged, falling 0.46% (-237.33 pts) to 51,285.73.
- It was nonetheless a losing week for Wall Street: the Nasdaq dropped 3.4%, the S&P 500 fell 2.7% and the Dow slipped 0.9%, with Thursday's roughly 5.5% Nasdaq plunge (its worst session in about 18 months, pushing the index into correction territory) the dominant driver.
- Thursday's selloff followed Democratic midterm wins that raised fears of tighter curbs on US data-centre construction; Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said data-centre activity should pause until stronger protections are in place, keeping AI-infrastructure policy risk in focus heading into the weekend.
- A Bloomberg report said Microsoft is winding down a deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle, and CNBC reported Microsoft is also considering cutting the number of "neocloud" partners it works with — both seen as incremental signs of AI-capex discipline rather than retreat.
- October's US non-farm payrolls rose 165,000, well above the 59,000 forecast, while the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, giving the Fed and markets a more resilient read on the labour market even as AI-sector valuation concerns persist.
- Individual-stock moves were sharp: Microchip Technology fell 14% on weak guidance, while IonQ jumped 17% after raising its revenue guidance — underscoring continued dispersion within the chip/AI-adjacent complex.
- The Australian dollar extended its slide into the weekend, with AUD/USD closing Friday's New York session at 0.6866 (down from Thursday's 0.6930 close), while gold edged up 0.52% to $4,065.97/oz; no ASX session or Australian economic releases occurred on Saturday Nov 7 itself, with Monday's reopen the next test of sentiment.

## Investment Implications

- A stronger-than-expected October payrolls print, alongside a down week for equities, reinforces a "good news is complicated news" dynamic: resilient US jobs data reduces near-term recession risk but could also temper expectations for further Fed easing.
- AUD/USD's slide to 0.6866 into the weekend, despite the RBA's Nov 3 rate hike, points to broader US-dollar strength and global risk sentiment currently dominating over the local rate differential — worth monitoring when FX markets reopen Sunday evening US time/Monday morning in Asia.
