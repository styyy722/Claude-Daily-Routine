# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-09-29

## Executive Summary
- US equities fell for a second straight session as Treasury yields pushed to fresh multi-decade highs, with the 30-year hitting its highest level since 2002-04: S&P 500 -0.16% to 7,670.84, Dow -0.26% (-131.59pts) to 51,349.92, Nasdaq Composite -0.09% to 26,797.54.
- The RBA raised its cash rate by 25bp to 4.60% — the fourth hike of 2026 and the highest level since November 2011 — citing elevated inflation and a broadening Middle East conflict pushing global energy prices above August forecasts; further hikes were flagged as possible.
- The ASX 200 shrugged off the hike to close 0.3% higher at 8,709, led by a 4.6% surge in Information Technology, even as AUD/USD whipsawed and fell to nine-week lows below 0.7000 after Governor Bullock signalled less urgency on further tightening.

## Key Financial News
- **RBA delivers fourth 2026 hike, warns more may follow**: The Board raised the cash rate 25bp to 4.60%, the highest since November 2011, pointing to elevated inflation (currently ~3.5%, above the 2-3% target band) and a broadening Middle East conflict that has pushed global energy prices well above August forecasts; the statement flagged further tightening as possible. ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-27.html))
- **Wall Street logs a second losing session**: The S&P 500 (-0.16%), Dow (-0.26%) and Nasdaq Composite (-0.09%) all slipped as another leg up in Treasury yields weighed on sentiment, with the 30-year yield cited as reaching its highest level since the early-2000s. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-september-29-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080526442.html))
- **ASX 200 shrugs off the hike, tech surges**: The index closed 0.3% higher at 8,709 as Information Technology jumped 4.6% and Materials added 1%, with commentary noting the rate rise was largely priced in (~92% market-implied probability beforehand); the yield-sensitive Real Estate sector clawed back most of an earlier 1.7% intraday loss. ([Stockhead](https://stockhead.com.au/news/closing-bell-bullock-hikes-tech-rips-and-copper-lurks-near-records/))
- **AUD/USD whipsaws to a nine-week low**: The currency initially rallied on the hawkish-sounding statement but reversed after Governor Bullock's press conference was read as signalling less urgency on further hikes, falling below 0.7000 in European trade despite the RBA move. ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/the-rba-hiked-and-sounded-hawkish-the-australian-dollar-fell-anyway-heres-why-202609291415))
- **Gold hovers near a two-month low**: Gold opened near its weakest level in roughly two months before recovering somewhat intraday, as elevated bond yields continue to weigh on non-yielding bullion. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-price-today-tuesday-september-29-2026-gold-opens-near-2-month-low-but-brent-crude-also-moving-lower-104439751.html))
- **Brent little changed, WTI range-bound**: Brent crude edged up marginally to $105.31/bbl while WTI traded in a roughly $90.76–$94.72/bbl range, holding near recent levels after Monday's Iran-driven spike. ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil))
- **Iron ore stays under pressure**: With no fresh Tuesday print confirmed, the market remains anchored near the ~$96.6/T (Qingdao 62% Fe) level last confirmed Friday, consistent with reports that Chinese iron ore prices fell back below $100/T through late September. ([GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/news/iron-ore-prices-fell-back-below-100-per-tonne-at-the-end-of-september/))

## Investment Implications
- The RBA's hike-plus-hawkish-caveat combination, paired with AUD/USD's failure to hold gains, suggests markets read the accompanying guidance as less committal than the rate move itself implied — a pattern likely to keep the currency choppy around future data releases.
- Persistent upward pressure on long-dated US Treasury yields (30-year near multi-decade highs) continues to be the dominant headwind for US equities, arguing for continued caution on rate-sensitive and long-duration growth names until yields stabilise.
- The ASX 200's ability to rally through a rate hike — led by technology rather than defensives this time — points to improving risk appetite locally, though the sharp intraday swing in Real Estate underscores that yield-sensitive sectors remain fragile to the rate path.
