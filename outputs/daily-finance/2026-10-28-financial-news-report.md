## Key Financial News
- **Fed holds rates, but with a historic split dissent**: The FOMC voted 10-2 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.75%–4.00%. Governor Lisa Cook (her first dissent since joining the Board in 2022) and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both preferred a 25bp hike — the first time a governor and a regional bank president have dissented together on the same side since October 1992. The Fed's statement upgraded its description of economic activity from "solid" to "expanding at a strong pace," while September CPI remained elevated at 3.7% year-over-year.
- **Chair Warsh cautious, non-committal on December**: In his press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh said the committee judged that its "monetary medicine" is starting to work but drew no firm conclusions, and said politics played no role in the decision ahead of the midterm elections. He reiterated he does not believe in forward guidance "absent exigent circumstances." Ahead of the meeting, CME FedWatch had priced roughly a 78% chance of a December rate hike.
- **Equities take the hold calmly, eyes turn to Big Tech earnings**: The S&P 500 (+0.28%), Nasdaq Composite (+0.27%) and Dow (+0.19%) all closed modestly higher, suggesting the hold itself was broadly priced in. Attention now shifts to Alphabet and Microsoft, which were both due to report earnings after Wednesday's close — a key test for the AI-led rally that has driven recent sessions, following Tuesday's chip-sector gains.
- **Oil rises, gold firms**: WTI crude rose 2.04% to roughly $101.94/bbl, continuing the volatility seen over the past week amid ongoing Middle East supply-risk headlines flagged in recent sessions (no new day-specific catalyst could be independently verified for today). Gold edged up 0.17% to around $4,067/oz, a modest safe-haven bid around the Fed's unusually split decision.
- **Treasury yields little changed**: The US 10-year yield held near 5.02% (+0.02pp), broadly steady as the market digested a hold that came with hawkish dissents on one side and a cautious, data-dependent chair on the other.
- **ASX steady, AUD firms ahead of RBA**: The ASX 200 was essentially flat (-0.03% to 8,847.30) while AUD/USD rose 0.41% to ~0.7053, drawing some support from a softer US dollar tone around the Fed decision. The RBA's cash rate stands at 4.60% following its September 29 hike, with its next decision due November 3; an Australian Q3 CPI print was reported as due around this date by at least one source, but a reliable, independently verifiable figure could not be confirmed, so no specific number is reported here.
- **Iron ore flat**: Iron ore held steady at roughly $90.70/tonne, unchanged on the day, with no major China-demand headlines identified for the session.

## Investment Implications
- The joint governor/regional-president dissent — the first since 1992 — signals growing internal division at the Fed over whether policy is restrictive enough, raising the stakes for the December meeting and any data released between now and then.
- The muted equity reaction (all three major US indices moved less than 0.3%) suggests markets had largely priced in a hold; the more important catalyst is Alphabet and Microsoft's earnings after today's close, which could set the tone for the AI trade into Thursday.
- A firmer AUD/USD into the RBA's November 3 decision reflects diverging near-term central bank paths — a Fed that held with hawkish dissents versus an RBA that already hiked in September — and is worth watching for follow-through as more Australian inflation data becomes available.
