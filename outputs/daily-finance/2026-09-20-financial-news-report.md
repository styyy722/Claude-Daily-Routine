## Key Financial News

- **Houthis strike Riyadh and Aramco sites near Yanbu**: Houthi forces claimed attacks on oil depots near Riyadh airport and on Saudi Aramco infrastructure in Yanbu on Sunday, in stated retaliation for Saudi strikes on Sanaa; Saudi and Gulf equity markets fell on the news and President Trump reportedly cut a trip short in response to the escalation. ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/20/live-saudi-aramco-iran-war/))
- **Iran hardens stance on Hormuz**: Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz "will remain closed until Iran's conditions are met," even as Iran's security chief separately conveyed conditions for ending the conflict to Washington and Foreign Minister Araghchi departed for the UN General Assembly in New York via Qatar.
- **Oil eyes a rebound after a flat Friday**: WTI and Brent both eased late last week — WTI down to roughly $95.46/bbl (about 10% off this month's high) and Brent near $103.83-103.90/bbl (off a monthly high near $108-110) — but analysts flagged the weekend Houthi attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure as a likely catalyst for a renewed push higher when trading resumes. ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/09/20/crude-oil-brent-and-wti-eye-rebound-as-houthi-and-saudi-clashes-escalate/))
- **East-West pipeline still offline**: Saudi Arabia's ~7M bpd East-West pipeline, shut since 11 September after drone attacks, remains closed; some analysts now believe a full restoration could take months rather than the "days" originally suggested by US officials, extending reliance on the Strait of Hormuz and alternative routing.
- **UAE signals preference for dialogue**: UAE official Anwar Gargash indicated the Emirates continues to seek dialogue with Iran while protecting its own national interests, reflecting the wider Gulf balancing act between de-escalation and solidarity with Saudi Arabia.
- **Wall Street closed last week's Fed-hike volatility mixed**: Friday's session left the S&P 500 +0.17% at 7,650.50 and Nasdaq Composite +0.39% at 26,522.55, while the Dow fell 0.18% to 51,682.64; the US 10-year Treasury yield held near 5.00% after touching a post-2007 high of 5.041% mid-week.
- **RBA decision now nine days out**: The RBA's 29 September meeting remains the key domestic catalyst, with markets still pricing elevated odds of a hike after Governor Michele Bullock's hawkish testimony; consensus among major banks is split between a September move (NAB) and November (ANZ, CBA, Westpac).

## Investment Implications
- The weekend escalation — Houthi strikes on Riyadh and Yanbu, plus Iran's hardened Hormuz rhetoric — raises the odds of a fresh geopolitical risk premium in oil and safe-haven demand in gold when Asian and Gulf markets reopen.
- With the East-West pipeline restoration timeline now looking longer than initially indicated, energy-sensitive and inflation-linked assets may stay volatile into the new week regardless of Friday's modest oil pullback.
- Australian and US cash markets remain closed through the weekend, so Monday's reopen will need to price in both the Fed's rate hike from last week and this weekend's Middle East developments simultaneously; the RBA's 29 September decision remains the key domestic event to watch.
