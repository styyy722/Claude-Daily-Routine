## Key Financial News
- Anthropic (ANT) made its Nasdaq debut after pricing its IPO at $83/share — above its marketed $70–$78 range — raising a record $81.3 billion and valuing the company above $1.5 trillion at the first trade; shares opened at $111.50 (+34%), touched an intraday high near $115, then eased to roughly the mid-to-high $90s (still well above the IPO price) as the broader market weakened through the session.
- US stocks extended their recent slide Thursday afternoon, with AMD falling about 9% after research firm Hunterbrook disclosed a short position citing China exposure, and Nvidia also trading lower; UnitedHealth remained under pressure following Wednesday's 10.3% plunge tied to a proposed CMS "CRUSH" rule that would expand audits of insurers.
- The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) jumped roughly 10–11% to around 27, moving further into "high fear" territory, as hopes that Anthropic's listing would revive broader AI-sector sentiment failed to broaden into a market-wide rally.
- Wednesday's October FOMC minutes showed "almost all" officials judged further interest-rate increases likely appropriate in the months ahead, citing AI-driven demand as a material inflation risk; Fed Governor Christopher Waller flagged a possible half-point hike at the December 8–9 meeting.
- Talks between President Trump and President Xi concluded with little reported progress on semiconductor export controls, rare earths, or tariffs.
- Walmart beat Q3 earnings estimates and raised its 2026 outlook, with e-commerce sales up 17%, one of the few bright spots in an otherwise weak session; JPMorgan also disclosed that some third-party vendors were targeted in a cyberattack, with no disruption to its own services.
- The ASX 200 fell 1.04% (-91.4 points) to an 8,730.4 close — a 20-day low and its sixth straight losing session, wiping out the index's 2026 year-to-date gains — led by gold miners, base-metals miners and energy stocks (South32 -5.9%), while telcos, tech and healthcare held up better; the slide came as October unemployment jumped to 4.9% (highest since October 2021), prompting AMP, Betashares and HSBC economists to flag rising recession risk, even as some (Moody's) argued the weakness remains concentrated at the margins.

## Investment Implications
- Anthropic's volatile debut — strong initial pop, then a steady fade as the broader market sold off — suggests investor appetite for AI-linked assets remains fragile even when a marquee listing draws heavy demand (reports put the deal roughly 6x oversubscribed); a soft finish to its first session would be a weak signal for AI-sector sentiment more broadly.
- Hawkish Fed signalling (a possible December rate rise) alongside a ~5% 10-year Treasury yield continues to pressure equity valuations and support the US dollar, a headwind for AUD/USD, which touched a 10-month low intraday.
- Australia's weakening labour market data is increasing economist talk of recession risk, which is weighing on domestic risk assets even as most major banks still expect the RBA to remain on hold rather than cut in the near term.
