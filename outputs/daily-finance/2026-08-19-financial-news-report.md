## Key Financial News

- **Treasury buyback lifts bonds:** The US Treasury announced it would increase buybacks of long-dated government debt (10-to-30-year sector) by "at least double," beginning September 9 through November 4, which eased the recent buyers' strike and pulled yields lower — the 10-year yield fell about 5 basis points to 4.65% and the 30-year dropped 9 basis points to 5.19%, retreating from its highest level since 2007 earlier in the week.
- **Equities snap losing streak:** The S&P 500 rose 0.21% to 7,708, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.16% to 26,331, and the Dow added 0.22% to 53,463, ending a three-day slide as bond-market pressure eased.
- **Moderna's best day ever:** Moderna shares surged roughly 177% after positive Phase 3 data showed its intismeran autogene, developed with Merck, paired with Merck's Keytruda produced strong results against melanoma; Merck shares rose about 13% on hopes the therapy could extend to other cancer types.
- **Fed minutes flag rate-hike risk:** Minutes from the latest Federal Reserve meeting showed several officials supportive of raising rates further if inflation fails to decline, keeping policy uncertainty elevated even as bond yields eased.
- **Tariff developments:** President Trump postponed threatened 50% tariffs on Canadian goods for three days pending further negotiations.
- **Oil firms:** Brent crude rose about $1.18 to around $93.60/barrel amid ongoing Strait of Hormuz-related supply concerns.
- **ASX 200 dips modestly:** The index fell 0.18%, lagging the improving US bond-market backdrop, as investors continued to digest a busy local earnings season.

## Investment Implications

- The Treasury's expanded buyback program signals a deliberate policy effort to cap long-dated yields, which could provide near-term relief for rate-sensitive equities and reduce volatility in fixed income if sustained.
- Moderna and Merck's cancer-vaccine breakthrough highlights biotech as a potential source of idiosyncratic, event-driven equity gains independent of the broader macro backdrop.
- Persistent hawkish signals from Fed officials, even as yields ease on technical buyback support, suggest markets should not assume near-term rate cuts are imminent.
