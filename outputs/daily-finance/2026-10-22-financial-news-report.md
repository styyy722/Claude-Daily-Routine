## Key Financial News
- **AI-spending doubts hit the Nasdaq hard**: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC that capital for the AI investment boom was becoming "scarce," citing higher interest rates and a glut of existing spending commitments; this reignited broader investor worries that data-centre construction could slow as borrowing costs rise, dragging the Nasdaq down 2.60% in its worst session since early June, per AP and NBC News.
- **Mixed mega-cap earnings reactions**: Meta beat earnings and revenue forecasts only after excluding billions in legal penalties paid the prior quarter, warned it will lift 2027 capital spending to meet computing demand, and disclosed an additional $825bn in planned AI-infrastructure contracts — its shares fell on the report. Tesla said it was scaling back its own capital-spending plans, disappointing investors hoping for more aggressive expansion, and its shares also fell. Netflix fell 5.3% despite a stronger-than-expected profit, on concerns its growth momentum may be slowing.
- **Oil spikes on fresh Strait of Hormuz incident and Iran-strike reports**: Iranian state television reported seven oil tankers caught fire and were damaged near the Strait of Hormuz — potentially the most vessels damaged there in a single day since the Iran war began on February 28, if confirmed. Separately, reports (via the New York Times) said Israel and the US are preparing to resume strikes on Iran, likely targeting energy infrastructure and nuclear sites, after the November 3 US midterm elections; US military and diplomatic personnel were reported being evacuated from several Middle East countries, and Iranian officials threatened retaliation against regional data centres and undersea internet cables. Brent crude rose roughly 3–4% to around $112–113/bbl, its highest since May.
- **Treasuries rally as safe-haven flows return**: Demand for US government bonds rose as investors rotated out of riskier assets including tech stocks, reversing a multi-week selling trend; the 10-year Treasury yield fell below 5% for the first time since late September.
- **RBA flags a worsening inflation outlook**: RBA Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser said tighter policy is working but the inflation outlook is "materially worse," citing September headline CPI at 4% and elevated oil prices; market-implied odds of a November 3 rate hike roughly doubled to about 64%. Third-quarter CPI data due next week is expected to show core inflation near 3.5% y/y, above the RBA's 2–3% target band.
- **Asian tech stocks sold off sharply**: SoftBank Group fell 6.9%, Tokyo Electron fell 7%, Advantest fell 6%, SK Hynix fell 5.5% and Samsung Electronics fell 4%, mirroring Wall Street's AI-related weakness; Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.7%, South Korea's Kospi fell 3.5%, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 3%.
- **ASX 200 slips on global risk-off tone plus local names**: The ASX 200 fell 0.43% to 8,793.20 as consumer discretionary, mining and energy stocks led losses; Fortescue fell 4.5% amid reports over its cash balance and talks with a China state-backed iron ore buyer, while Codan dropped 8.1% after a roughly A$312 million off-market block trade. Traders were cautious ahead of Friday's flash October PMI data.

## Investment Implications
- Huang's "scarce capital" comment, following mixed-to-disappointing capex and earnings signals from Meta and Tesla, suggests the market is becoming more discriminating about AI-related spending commitments versus near-term cash generation — a dynamic likely to keep volatility elevated in AI-exposed names into the next earnings wave and the October 27–28 FOMC meeting.
- Oil above $112/bbl (Brent) on an active Iran conflict and a fresh Strait of Hormuz incident is a live inflation and growth risk that could complicate central-bank policy paths in the US, Australia and elsewhere if sustained, even as falling Treasury yields signal markets are currently pricing greater safe-haven demand than inflation risk.
- A more hawkish RBA tone (Hauser's inflation warning, odds of a November hike near 64%) raises the prospect of tighter Australian monetary policy into year-end, a potential headwind for rate-sensitive ASX sectors, while next week's Q3 CPI print is a key swing factor for that November 3 decision.
