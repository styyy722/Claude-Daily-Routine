# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-07-14

## Executive Summary
- US stocks closed higher after June CPI came in cooler than expected (3.5% y/y vs. 3.8% forecast), cutting the market-implied odds of a July Fed rate hike to roughly 20% from over 40%.
- Escalating US-Iran tensions in the Strait of Hormuz — fresh US airstrikes and Iranian attacks on shipping — pushed oil above $80/barrel for the first time in a month.
- IBM shares fell 25% on a profit warning citing soft software and infrastructure demand, dragging on the Dow even as chip stocks rallied.

## Key Financial News
- June US CPI rose 3.5% annualized, below the 3.8% consensus, easing inflation concerns and boosting Treasuries and equities.
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh testified to Congress on the semiannual Monetary Policy Report, reaffirming commitment to restoring price stability; the fed funds rate remains at 3.50%-3.75% since June.
- Semiconductor stocks rebounded sharply from the prior session's sell-off, with the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) up about 2.5%.
- IBM slid 25% after warning of lower Q2 profit on weak software and infrastructure demand, the biggest drag on the Dow.
- US-Iran conflict intensified for a sixth consecutive day: US CENTCOM struck Iranian military targets while Iran's IRGC claimed responsibility for disabling two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, disrupting shipping traffic.
- ASX 200 closed flat at 8,808.5 as investors weighed Middle East risk against a steady local session; decliners outnumbered advancers 619 to 422.
- AUD/USD gained 0.52% to ~0.6952, supported by the softer US inflation print and its dovish implications for Fed policy.
- The RBA made no policy announcement on July 14; its next rate decision is scheduled for 10–11 August 2026.

## Investment Implications
- A cooler US inflation print reduces near-term Fed rate-hike risk, which is broadly supportive for risk assets and rate-sensitive sectors, though geopolitical risk remains a wildcard.
- The Middle East conflict's impact on oil and shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz could feed back into inflation if sustained, complicating the Fed's path.
- Company-specific risk (e.g., IBM's guidance cut) is diverging from the broader tech rally, underscoring the importance of earnings season stock selection over broad index exposure.

## Watchlist for Today
- Further developments in the US-Iran conflict and any disruption to Strait of Hormuz shipping, which could keep oil prices elevated.
- Additional bank and corporate earnings reports for confirmation or contradiction of IBM's soft demand signal.
- Fed commentary following Chair Warsh's congressional testimony for further clues on the July policy meeting.
