## Key Financial News
- **US equities**: The S&P 500 (-0.2%), Nasdaq Composite (-0.3%) and Dow (-0.2%) all slipped Friday, pausing after the S&P 500 set an intraday record of 7,816.70 and a closing record above 7,798 earlier in the week. All three indices still closed higher for the week — the S&P 500's third consecutive weekly gain.
- **Consumer sentiment**: A preliminary August University of Michigan survey showed Americans grew more pessimistic about the economy, with inflation remaining a top concern — the main driver of Friday's afternoon pullback.
- **Australian market**: The ASX 200 closed down 0.80% (9,115.2) on Friday, with mining and resource stocks leading declines as metals prices softened.
- **RBA policy**: The Reserve Bank of Australia held its cash rate at 4.35% for a second consecutive month, noting labour market conditions have "eased by a little more than expected." It revised its unemployment forecast up, now seeing a rise to 4.5% by end-2026 and a peak of 4.8% by mid-2028.
- **Bond yields**: The US 10-year Treasury yield sat around 4.68%–4.70%, with rate-cut expectations for the Fed's September meeting remaining a key swing factor for yields.
- **FX**: AUD/USD rose 0.33% to 0.7083, supported by broader risk sentiment even as the ASX fell.
- **Commodities**: WTI crude settled up 1.4% at $82.40/bbl after the US threatened tighter sanctions and "economic isolation" of Iran, raising fears of disruption near the Strait of Hormuz. Gold eased from a 10-week high to around $4,365/oz as profit-taking followed a recent inflation-driven rally.
- **China**: Q2 GDP growth slowed to 4.3% YoY (from 5.0% in Q1), with industrial output up 5.4% in H1 2026 but retail sales growth a soft 1.0% YoY in June, underscoring a persistent gap between factory output and household demand.

## Investment Implications
- The pause in US equities after record highs, combined with softer consumer sentiment, suggests markets may be entering a more cautious, data-dependent phase ahead of the Fed's September meeting.
- Renewed Middle East supply risk (Iran sanctions rhetoric) is a factor that could keep energy prices — and headline inflation readings — elevated, a watch point for both the Fed and RBA.
- Australia's soft labour market outlook and China's uneven growth (strong industrial output, weak consumption) are headwinds for AUD-sensitive and resources-heavy portfolios, even as near-term AUD/USD strength persists.
