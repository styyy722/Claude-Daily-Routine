## Executive Summary
- A hotter-than-expected October US CPI print (headline +0.4% m/m vs +0.3% forecast; core +0.3% vs +0.2% forecast) pushed Wall Street lower on Tuesday (S&P 500 -0.38% to 7,520.93, Dow -0.30% to 52,231.89, Nasdaq Composite -0.74% to 25,065.88), with markets now fully pricing a December Fed rate hike; the BLS could not publish 12-month inflation rates due to a government-shutdown-related data gap.

## Key Financial News
- US October CPI ran hot: headline +0.4% m/m (vs +0.3% forecast, +0.6% in September) and core +0.3% m/m (vs +0.2% forecast), a third straight 0.3% core print. The BLS could not publish annual (12-month) rates for October because the comparable October 2025 data was never collected during the shutdown; the last published annual rates remain 3.7% headline / 2.6% core (September). Markets responded by fully pricing in a December Fed rate hike, and the 2-year Treasury yield jumped roughly 17 basis points.
- Wall Street fell on the CPI read: S&P 500 -0.38% to 7,520.93, Dow -0.30% to 52,231.89, Nasdaq Composite -0.74% to 25,065.88, giving back part of Monday's sharp rebound. Tech led the decline and Nvidia fell further, though the equal-weight S&P 500 held up better, pointing to narrower, mega-cap-led weakness rather than a broad risk-off move.
- The ASX 200 closed +0.27% (+24.2 pts) at 9,153.2, an 11-week high, led by tech and base-metals stocks; Life360 jumped 10.1% in a rebound, while PEXA fell after cutting guidance and NEXTDC dropped 4.0%. Banks were mixed and weighed on the index as a group.
- Commonwealth Bank's Q1 trading update showed unaudited cash profit of $2.8bn (flat on the FY26 second-half quarterly average, +8% y/y), but loan impairment expense of $300m came in above expectations and home-loan arrears rose 7 bps to 0.8%; CBA shares fell ~1.2% and J.P. Morgan flagged possible modest FY27 profit downgrade risk.
- New home lending data showed a record 15.5% fall in value and 15% fall in volume for the September quarter, with investor lending down 32.3% in value; the ABS attributed the slump partly to tax changes and expectations of softer house prices, beyond the effect of borrowing costs.
- David Jones' receivers announced the closure of 19 of its roughly 38 stores, affecting about 6,000 employees, while continuing to pursue a sale or recapitalisation of the department-store chain. Separately, Macquarie Bank agreed to a proposed $14m penalty for breaching the Corporations Act over its "Welcome Rate" savings promotion, on top of $8.8m in remediation already paid.
- AUD/USD firmed modestly to 68.78 US cents (+0.2%) after dipping to 68.63 cents early in the session; gold fell as much as 2.7% to around US$4,026/oz immediately after the hot CPI print before recovering to roughly US$4,045/oz (+0.5%) by late afternoon. ANZ expects the RBA to pause at 4.85% following this month's 25bp hike, though a further move in December has not been ruled out.

## Investment Implications
- A hot CPI print with no official year-on-year comparison (due to the shutdown data gap) adds genuine uncertainty to how markets and the Fed assess the underlying inflation trend into the December meeting — headline risk from Fed commentary is likely to rise over coming weeks.
- The divergence between a weaker cap-weighted Wall Street session and a steadier equal-weight S&P suggests the sell-off was concentrated in mega-cap tech/AI names rather than broad-based risk aversion, which is consistent with the ASX's ability to rally on domestic strength despite the soft US lead.
- Australian housing and bank data (record home-lending declines, CBA's in-line but softer-than-hoped margin/arrears trends) point to a cooling domestic credit cycle that bears watching alongside the RBA's post-hike pause signal.
