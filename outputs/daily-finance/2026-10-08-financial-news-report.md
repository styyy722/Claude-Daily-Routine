# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-10-08

## Executive Summary
- US tech stocks fell for a second straight session after the Financial Times reported OpenAI's annualized revenue is roughly $20 billion below prior estimates, triggering a sharp AI/chip selloff (Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down as much as 4% intraday; Arm, Intel and Marvell each fell more than 6%); the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.25% while the Dow bucked the trend, rising 0.1% as money rotated out of AI names.

- Oil whipsawed on Middle East headlines: Brent briefly topped $104/bbl on reports the US was weighing further strikes on Iran plus Gulf supply disruption from a tropical storm, then pared gains after President Trump said the US would not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms; WTI still finished the day sharply higher.

## Key Financial News
- **AI/chip selloff on OpenAI revenue report**: A Financial Times report that OpenAI's annualized revenue is about $20 billion short of previously signaled levels hit AI-linked names hard; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell as much as 4% intraday before paring losses, with Arm, Intel and Marvell each down more than 6% and Micron off more than 5%. Nvidia also declined.
- **Nasdaq falls for second straight day**: The Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.25% to 27,193.34 and the S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36, both pulling back from record highs set earlier in the week; the Dow Jones was the exception, adding 0.10% to 51,231.64 as investors rotated toward less AI-exposed names.
- **Treasury yields hit multi-decade high before easing**: The 10-year yield spiked to 5.36% intraday — the highest level since 2002 — before settling back to roughly 5.23-5.24%; the 30-year yield reached 5.61%. Yields remain elevated following the hawkish September FOMC minutes, which showed most officials expect another hike before year-end.
- **Oil swings on Iran and storm headlines**: Brent crude briefly topped $104/bbl on reports the US was weighing further strikes on Iran and on Gulf production disruption from a tropical storm, before paring gains after President Trump said the US would not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections; WTI still closed sharply higher on the day.
- **ASX 200 pulls back**: Australia's benchmark fell 0.77% to 8,660.90, giving back part of its recent record run, consistent with the softer Wall Street lead and persistently elevated global bond yields; AUD/USD eased 0.32% to 0.6960 on broad US dollar firmness.
- **Gold edges higher despite high yields**: Gold futures rose 0.40% to $4,157.20/oz, suggesting some safe-haven demand from the tech selloff and Middle East tensions partially offset the usual drag from higher real yields.
- **Labor market and risk assets**: US initial jobless claims fell to 197,000, a sign of continued labor-market resilience. Bitcoin fell about 2% to roughly $81,000, a three-week low, with crypto-linked equities such as Coinbase also lower, reflecting the broader risk-off tone.

## Investment Implications
- The selloff is concentrated in AI/semiconductor names tied to a single data point (OpenAI's revenue shortfall) rather than a broad macro shock, so its durability will depend on whether further scrutiny of AI-sector revenue and spending assumptions follows in coming sessions.
- Treasury yields touching a 2002-era high, even briefly, keeps upward pressure on borrowing costs and equity valuations, particularly for growth and AI-linked stocks; the 27-28 October FOMC meeting is now a key catalyst for whether this yield move extends or reverses.
- Oil's volatility around Iran-related headlines is a reminder that geopolitical risk premium can reappear quickly; sustained high energy prices alongside high yields would be a double headwind for risk assets, worth watching into the Fed meeting.
