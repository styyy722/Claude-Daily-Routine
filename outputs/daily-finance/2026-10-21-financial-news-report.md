## Key Financial News
- **AI-trade rally on tech earnings**: Global risk assets firmed after strong technology-sector earnings revived confidence in the AI trade, with South Korea's Kospi up 2.6%, Taiwan's Taiex up 0.9%, and Nasdaq 100 futures adding 0.7% ahead of the US open, per Tickmill. Investors are reportedly "actively penalizing execution risks and capex burdens," meaning companies are being rewarded for showing actual revenue and cash-flow progress rather than capex headlines alone.
- **Strait of Hormuz shipping risk**: Fresh attacks on commercial vessels near the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent crude above $110/bbl, with Tickmill noting that persistently high oil above that level threatens the broader global disinflation narrative; WTI's own technical outlook was described as bullish with support near $95.74/bbl.
- **Bond yields and the dollar**: US Treasury yields edged lower on the day (the 10-yr nonetheless remains near a multi-decade-high zone around 5.1%) as traders weighed growth risks against energy-driven price pressure; the US Dollar Index traded softer, with recent AI-stock-related volatility seen reducing near-term Fed-tightening expectations.
- **Gold defensive**: Spot gold traded cautiously as resilient real yields and a partial unwind of safe-haven flows capped gains, even against the Hormuz-related risk backdrop.
- **RBA and AUD/USD**: An RBA deputy governor said Australia's inflation outlook has worsened despite already-tighter policy; Goldman Sachs now forecasts a 25bp RBA hike to 4.85% at the November meeting. AUD/USD firmed modestly, with technical commentary flagging a possible reversal risk near Fibonacci resistance around 0.7026.
- **Big US earnings week in focus**: Tesla was scheduled to report third-quarter results after Wednesday's close (analyst consensus around $0.43 adjusted EPS on ~$27.8bn revenue, per Zacks), with the market reaction due to show up in Thursday's session; Netflix and Meta were also among the companies reporting this week, as part of a heavy October 19–23 earnings slate that markets are treating as a key test of AI-spending discipline. Specific Netflix/Tesla post-earnings share-price reactions for this week could not be independently confirmed as of this report and are not included to avoid citing unverified figures.
- **Local markets firmer**: The ASX 200 edged up 0.16% to 8,831.30 while AUD/USD rose 0.36% to ~0.7028 during Sydney trade; iron ore was essentially flat and gold ticked higher in the Trading Economics CFD feed.

## Investment Implications
- The tech-led global rally suggests renewed, but more selective, risk appetite in AI-exposed names — markets appear to be differentiating between companies demonstrating real revenue/cash-flow delivery and those seen as carrying elevated execution or capex risk, making this week's remaining mega-cap earnings (including Tesla's report after today's close) a key swing factor.
- Oil above $110/bbl (Brent) on Strait of Hormuz disruption risk is a live source of inflation and growth uncertainty that could complicate central-bank policy paths in both the US and Australia if sustained, even as softer Treasury yields and a weaker dollar point to near-term easing expectations elsewhere.
- A hawkish shift in RBA rhetoric (deputy governor's inflation warning, Goldman's 25bp November hike call) raises the prospect of tighter Australian monetary policy into year-end, a potential headwind for rate-sensitive ASX sectors even as the index itself traded higher today.
