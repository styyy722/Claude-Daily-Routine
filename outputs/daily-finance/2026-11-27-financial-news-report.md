## Key Financial News
- US markets reopened for a shortened Black Friday session after Thursday's Thanksgiving closure: the S&P 500 rose 1.6% (+114.71 pts) to a confirmed 7,297.49, the Dow added 1.2% (+591.99 pts) to 50,774.94, and the Nasdaq Composite led with a 2.3% (+543.70 pt) gain to 24,129.50; the Russell 2000 added 1.7% to 2,750.30. All four posted their strongest weekly performance since April 2025, with the S&P 500 up 5.6%, the Dow up 5.5%, the Nasdaq up 6.7% and the Russell 2000 up 6.5% for the week.
- The rally was led by a tech and AI-linked rebound, with Micron up more than 7%; bank shares including JPMorgan Chase and gold miner Newmont also advanced, while Vertex Pharmaceuticals fell on an extended regulatory review of its kidney-disease treatment.
- Macro data reported alongside the rally: Q3 GDP growth was revised up to a 4.3% annualised pace, while October PCE inflation came in at 3.8% year-on-year (core 3.3%), a combination of strong growth and above-target inflation that leaves the Fed's policy path under scrutiny heading into December.
- The ASX 200 (confirmed close) jumped 70 points (+0.8%) to a one-week high of 8,834, its best week since early August (+2.8%), supported by strong US equity futures ahead of the Black Friday session, accelerating growth in China's October industrial profits, and Australia's newly announced Domestic Gas Reservation Scheme (requiring LNG exporters to reserve up to 20% of exports domestically from January 2028), which lifted hopes for lower east-coast energy costs.
- Gains on the ASX were tempered by China vowing retaliation against proposed new US tariffs; most sectors still advanced, led by industrials, financials, materials and technology.
- In single-stock news, WiseTech Global surged 14.5% after reaffirming its FY27 revenue targets, while energy producers Woodside Energy (+2.1%) and Santos (+2.0%) gained on firmer oil prices.

## Investment Implications
- The breadth of Friday's rally — US tech/AI, banks, and ASX industrials, financials, materials and tech all advancing together — suggests markets are, for now, looking past this month's earlier AI- and tariff-driven volatility, though a single shortened, thinly-traded holiday session warrants a cautious read.
- The combination of upwardly revised US GDP growth and above-target PCE inflation keeps the Fed's December decision finely balanced; further data between now and the meeting will likely move rate expectations more than Friday's holiday-session price action.
- Australia's Domestic Gas Reservation Scheme is a multi-year structural policy change rather than a near-term catalyst; its equity impact is likely to show up gradually in east-coast energy users and domestic gas producers rather than in Friday's session.
