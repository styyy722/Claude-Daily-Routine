## Key Financial News
- US equities closed out the week lower: Friday's session left the S&P 500 down 0.40% at 7,562 and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.91% at 25,136, while the Dow eked out a 0.10% gain to 52,348; the VIX volatility index rose 3.2% to 19.3, reflecting continued risk aversion after a volatile week.
- October producer prices rose 6.5% y/y (vs 6.6% expected, up from 6.3% in September) with core PPI at 5.0% y/y and the monthly headline +0.3% (vs +0.4% expected); combined with a hotter-than-expected CPI print earlier in the week, this cemented market pricing for a quarter-point Fed hike at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting, with some desks now also looking for a follow-up hike in January.
- The US 10-year Treasury yield briefly topped 5% intraweek and ended Friday near 5.15%; the 2-year yield jumped 42bps over the week to 4.88% (its worst week since 2024) and the 30-year rose to roughly 5.47%, as markets repriced for a more hawkish Fed path into 2027.
- The ASX 200 fell 0.7% to 9,082.0 on Friday (147 decliners vs 44 advancers) as the synchronized US/Australian bond-yield spike hit Materials and Financials. The broader market remains focused on the fallout from Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's confirmed resignation (CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO, director Harvey Jones the company's first board chair) following reports of alleged misconduct; shares remained volatile after last week's intraday swings of up to 14%.
- The RBA raised its cash rate 25bps to 4.85% on November 3 in a split 6–3 board vote — its fifth hike this year — and dropped explicit forward guidance on further tightening; the next decision is scheduled for December 8, keeping Australian front-end yields elevated alongside the US-led global bond selloff.
- AUD/USD held near a 10-month low around 0.6799 (Friday afternoon AEDT reading; no reliable Saturday-specific update was found given thin weekend forex liquidity), while gold eased 0.16% to roughly $3,874/oz and oil ended Friday little changed (WTI -0.20% to $88.56/bbl, Brent +0.02% to $95.84/bbl); iron ore was carried forward at roughly $91.6/tonne, as no updated Saturday print could be confirmed from the sources checked.
- Other data points from the week: Q3 US real GDP grew at a 3.4% annualized rate (delayed advance estimate following the earlier government shutdown), existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October to their lowest level since August 2010, and US household debt reached a record $19.1 trillion in Q3 per the New York Fed; by Friday, 91% of S&P 500 companies had reported Q3 earnings, with 84% beating estimates by an average surprise of 3.1%.
