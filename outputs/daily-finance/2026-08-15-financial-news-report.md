## Key Financial News

- **Consumer sentiment slumps:** The University of Michigan's preliminary August reading fell to 51 from July's 55.2, well below the 55 forecast, while year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.3% from 4.2%, unsettling investors already grappling with above-target inflation.
- **Retail sales disappoint:** July retail sales fell 0.6% month-over-month versus the 0.1% growth expected — the largest drop since May 2025 — complicating the Fed's policy calculus after recent softer inflation data.
- **Equities retreat from records:** The S&P 500, Nasdaq and small-cap Russell 2000 (-1.39%) all fell as investors booked profits following Thursday's record close; the S&P 500 still finished its third consecutive positive week.
- **Bond yields firm:** The US 10-year Treasury yield rose about 5 basis points to roughly 4.67%, while the 30-year yield stood near 5.27%, reflecting persistent inflation concerns.
- **Gold and crypto sell off sharply:** Spot gold dropped 2.88% to $4,529.90/oz and Bitcoin fell 3.11% to $77,427, as a firmer dollar and rising yields weighed on non-yielding assets.
- **Corporate movers:** Applied Materials shares fell more than 5% despite beating forecasts, on concerns guidance wasn't strong enough; Reddit was confirmed for S&P 500 inclusion effective 18 August; reports emerged of Workday exploring a possible transaction with Silver Lake.
- **ASX extends pullback:** The ASX 200 fell 0.92% to 9,115.2, its fifth decline in six sessions as mining and gold stocks led losses, extending the retreat from the index's 6 August record high.

## Investment Implications

- The combination of weak consumer sentiment, rising inflation expectations and soft retail sales raises the risk of a "stagflation" narrative gaining traction, which could pressure both equities and bonds simultaneously if it persists.
- Renewed strength in Treasury yields alongside a sharp gold sell-off suggests markets are, for now, leaning toward "higher for longer" rate expectations rather than imminent Fed easing.
- The ASX 200's fifth decline in six sessions signals waning momentum after its early-August record high, with mining and gold-sensitive names likely to stay volatile heading into the new week.
