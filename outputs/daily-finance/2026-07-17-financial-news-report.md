# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-07-17

## Executive Summary
- US stocks closed lower as a deepening global semiconductor sell-off and renewed US-Iran tensions hit sentiment; the S&P 500 and Nasdaq logged their worst week in months.
- Netflix shares plunged more than 10% after Q2 revenue of $12.56B narrowly missed the $12.6B consensus and the company guided to another quarter of slowing sales growth.
- Oil surged as Middle East tensions escalated, while Apple briefly overtook Nvidia as the world's most valuable company after a 3.5% drop in Nvidia shares.

## Key Financial News
- US equities fell broadly as the global tech and semiconductor sell-off deepened; the S&P 500 dropped 1.01%, the Nasdaq slid 1.4%, and the Dow lost 0.77%, capping a weekly loss of 1.6%, 2.9%, and 0.9% respectively.
- Semiconductor stocks were the biggest drag amid growing investor skepticism about whether massive AI infrastructure spending is sustainable and when it will generate returns.
- Netflix fell over 10% after Q2 revenue of $12.56B narrowly missed consensus and management flagged another quarter of slowing sales ahead.
- Sentiment was also hit by the debut of a new AI model from Chinese startup Moonshot, which the company claims performs on par with leading systems from OpenAI and Anthropic, stoking competitive concerns.
- Apple briefly reclaimed the title of world's most valuable company (~$4.9T market cap), surpassing Nvidia (~$4.86T) after Nvidia shares fell 3.5% in the chip sell-off.
- Oil prices jumped as tensions in the Middle East escalated further, with WTI crude gaining 3.7% to $81.88/barrel.
- ASX 200 closed 0.50% lower at 8,796.70, ending roughly flat for the week, as heavy selling in miners (including BHP) weighed on the index following softer operational updates.
- Iron ore (62% Fe, CFR China) rose to a one-month high of ~$101.85/T, supported by impending Chinese restrictions on select Fortescue portside cargoes that are expected to tighten domestic low-grade supply.
- The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% at its June meeting; the next FOMC decision is scheduled for July 28-29. The RBA made no announcement on July 17; its next decision is due 11 August 2026.

## Investment Implications
- The semiconductor sell-off signals growing market scrutiny of AI capex payback timelines — investors should expect continued volatility in AI-adjacent names pending clearer evidence of revenue conversion.
- Rising oil prices on Middle East escalation, alongside a steady 10-year yield, keep stagflation-adjacent risks on the radar heading into the July 28-29 FOMC meeting.
- Company-specific misses (Netflix) diverging from broader index moves reinforce that earnings-season stock selection matters more than broad beta exposure in the current environment.

## Watchlist for Today
- Further escalation (or de-escalation) in US-Iran tensions and its impact on oil prices and shipping.
- Additional mega-cap tech and semiconductor earnings for confirmation of AI capex spending trends.
- Commentary ahead of the July 28-29 FOMC meeting for signals on the rate path.
