## Executive Summary
- The ASX 200 surged 2.61% to a two-month high of 9,065.1 in Thursday's Sydney session, as a broad risk-on rally — sparked by Wall Street's post-midterm surge and a rebound in beaten-down commodity names — lifted miners (Materials +7.6%) even as banks lagged on a weak NAB result.

## Key Financial News
- Global equities extended their post-midterm relief rally: Wall Street's Wednesday close confirmed the Dow's biggest one-day gain in recent memory (+3.03% to 53,356.12), with the S&P 500 (+1.58%) and Nasdaq Composite (+1.44%) also firmly higher, after Democrats secured control of both chambers of Congress on Tuesday.
- The ASX 200 tracked the global risk-on mood, closing up 2.61% at 9,065.1 (All Ordinaries +2.8% to 9,205) — a two-month high — with Materials (XMJ) surging 7.6% as iron ore, lithium and rare-earths names rebounded broadly, while Financials (XFJ) slipped 0.3%.
- The RBA raised the cash rate by 25bp to 4.85% on Tuesday, its fifth hike of 2026; Bendigo Bank was among the first lenders to pass the increase on to variable home-loan customers.
- The US 10-year Treasury yield was reported around 4.95% in Thursday-morning (AEDT) coverage, a single-sourced reading that should be treated as indicative rather than confirmed.
- AUD/USD slipped 0.94% to 0.6938 even as domestic and global equities rallied, per the ABC News closing snapshot.
- Oil (WTI) crashed roughly 8–9% to a two-month low (around $86–88/bbl, its biggest one-day drop since April) as the midterm result raised bets that the Trump administration will wind down the Iran conflict; Gold rose 1.12% to $4,105/oz and iron ore added 0.7% to $93.75/t.
- In company news, NAB's FY26 cash profit fell 13.4% to $6.14bn (dividend held at $0.85) and shares dropped ~7.5% after it deferred roughly 2,500 flagged job cuts to 27 January 2027 under union pressure; David Jones, the 188-year-old department store chain, entered voluntary administration and receivership (McGrathNicol, FTI Consulting) after a landlord rent dispute, though stores continue trading; NEXTDC shares jumped ~49% after a Goodman Group/Digital Realty-linked entity built a stake at $9.50 in what the company called an "aftermarket share raid."

## Investment Implications
- The scale of both Wednesday's Wall Street rally and Thursday's ASX surge suggests markets are pricing in reduced political-gridlock risk and a lower probability of Iran-conflict escalation, but sharp single-day moves (oil -9%, Materials +7.6%) point to elevated volatility as positioning unwinds rather than a settled new trend.
- NAB's weaker result and David Jones's administration are reminders that parts of the domestic banking and retail sectors remain under pressure from high rates and soft consumer spending, even as the headline index rallies on global and commodity-driven factors.
- Gold's continued gains alongside a falling AUD suggest some investors are still hedging policy and currency uncertainty despite the broader risk-on tone.
