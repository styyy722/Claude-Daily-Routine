## Key Financial News
- US equities closed Friday with the S&P 500's seventh consecutive losing session (longest since February 2020); both the S&P 500 and Dow finished the day in technical correction, more than 10% below their respective August 5 and October 6 record closes.
- The sell-off centred on AI and semiconductor names: Nvidia fell roughly 14% to $125.53, its worst single-day decline since the DeepSeek-driven sell-off of January 2025, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 10.7% on Friday alone, extending its weekly loss to 17.13% — its worst week since September 2001.
- Anthropic's (ANT) Nasdaq debut earlier in the week, which had priced above range and opened sharply higher, failed to restore broader confidence in the AI trade; sentiment also remained clouded by leadership uncertainty at Nvidia following Jensen Huang's exit as CEO.
- The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) jumped 26.3% on Friday to 35.90 — its highest close since April 2025 — capping an 86% surge for the week, described by one market commentary as the largest weekly VIX jump in the dataset reviewed.
- The 10-year Treasury yield eased to roughly 4.95% (including post-market trading, after an intraday low of 4.91%), consistent with safe-haven bond buying accompanying the equity rout.
- Gold fell sharply, down 4.62% to $3,515.00/oz (including post-market trading) — its lowest close since August 2025 — an unusual move alongside a broad risk-off equity session that may reflect liquidation to raise cash rather than reduced safe-haven demand.
- Oil (WTI) swung from an intraday low of $80.78/bbl to close up 1.18% at $83.41/bbl, with the late bounce linked to reports that President Trump is weighing renewed military strikes against Iran.
- The ASX 200 closed Friday at a 100-day low of 8,592.60 (-1.58%), capping a 5.39% weekly decline as materials stocks fell 12.3% for the week; the Australian dollar slid to its weakest level of 2026 (below US$0.6700) after October employment data showed a surprise loss of 43,500 jobs and unemployment rising to 4.9% (highest since 2021), further denting RBA rate-hike expectations.

## Investment Implications
- The speed and breadth of the decline — a seventh straight losing session, a 26% single-day VIX spike, and simultaneous corrections in the S&P 500 and Dow — suggests markets are undergoing a disorderly repricing of AI infrastructure spending rather than reacting to a single data point; the unwind in chip stocks (SOX -17.13% for the week) has been the sharpest since 2001.
- Gold's decline alongside falling Treasury yields and a broad equity sell-off is an unusual combination that may reflect forced liquidation to cover losses elsewhere rather than a genuine shift in longer-term safe-haven demand; this divergence is worth monitoring once trading resumes.
- Oil's late bounce on Iran-related headlines adds a geopolitical risk premium to an already volatile macro backdrop; any escalation over the weekend could compound volatility when markets reopen Monday.
