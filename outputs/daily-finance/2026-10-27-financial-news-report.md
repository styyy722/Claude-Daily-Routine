# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-10-27

## Executive Summary
- US stocks rose Tuesday as chipmakers rallied on renewed AI optimism ahead of the Fed's rate decision and Wednesday's Alphabet/Microsoft earnings; the Nasdaq Composite led gains (+1.18%), with the S&P 500 and Dow also higher.
- The Fed's two-day FOMC meeting began Tuesday, with the rate decision and Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference due Wednesday afternoon; markets are broadly pricing a hold, though Warsh has given no clear signal of his leaning.
- Boeing shares slumped roughly 11% after the company lowered its delivery target — the day's biggest single-stock move — while gold jumped over 1% to above $4,000/oz and oil eased amid early, inconclusive diplomatic signals on Middle East tensions.

## Key Financial News
- **Chipmakers lead a broad AI-trade rally**: The Nasdaq Composite (+1.18%) outpaced the S&P 500 (+0.60%) and Dow (+0.03%) as semiconductor shares extended a prior-session rebound (the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had risen 2.8%); Broadcom, Micron, Intel, AMD and Nvidia were all higher in pre-market trading, with Nasdaq 100 futures implying around a 1.3% advance. The VIX fell roughly 5.5% to 18.39, signalling easing near-term risk aversion.
- **FOMC meeting begins, decision looms Wednesday**: The Fed's two-day policy meeting started Tuesday, with the rate decision due Wednesday afternoon alongside Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference. Markets are broadly pricing a hold (CME FedWatch had shown roughly 72% odds of no change), but Warsh has declined to preview the outcome. Alphabet and Microsoft report earnings Wednesday after the close — both a key test for the AI-led recovery and a potential swing factor for Wednesday's Fed reaction.
- **AI infrastructure financing headlines multiply**: Broadcom shares rose about 4% after a bank coalition and Blackstone arranged roughly $63bn in financing tied to Broadcom's chip sales to Anthropic. Meta's AI-infrastructure capital raise — part of a plan to raise up to $95bn, with an initial tranche of about $35bn split between roughly $22.5bn of common stock and $12.5bn of mandatory convertible preferred stock — was reported as heavily oversubscribed. SpaceX said it completed about $40bn in debt financing tied to AI-chip purchases, and Oracle expanded its OpenAI partnership while announcing new cloud services built on Cerebras Systems chips.
- **Boeing tumbles on delivery-target cut**: Boeing shares fell roughly 11% (to around $168) after the company lowered its delivery target, the market's largest single-stock decline of the session; UPS rose on a positive outlook, among Tuesday's other notable movers.
- **Oil eases, Treasuries little changed**: Brent crude held near $110/bbl (down about 0.6%) and WTI fell roughly 1.5% to ~$100/bbl as early, inconclusive diplomatic signals emerged around easing Middle East/Gulf tensions. The US 10-year Treasury yield was little changed near 5.0%.
- **Asia surges, Europe lags**: Asian markets rallied hard on the AI trade — South Korea's KOSPI jumped 3.1% (led by SK Hynix and Samsung) and Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.7% (led by Tokyo Electron and Advantest) — while European gains were muted: the Stoxx 600 rose 0.2% and the DAX/CAC 40 added roughly 0.3–0.4%, but the FTSE 100 fell 0.9% after a roughly 6% slide in HSBC on its results.
- **ASX and AUD firmer; RBA decision still ahead**: The ASX 200 added 0.46% to 8,850.10 and AUD/USD edged up to ~0.7025 (+0.12%). No single Australian-specific catalyst was identified for the day; the RBA's November 3 rate decision and the pending September-quarter CPI print remain the key near-term drivers for the currency and local equities.

## Investment Implications
- With the Fed's decision, Chair Warsh's press conference and Alphabet/Microsoft earnings all landing Wednesday, volatility risk is concentrated in the next 24 hours; Tuesday's rally reflects renewed optimism that could reverse quickly on a hawkish surprise or disappointing mega-cap results.
- The scale of AI-related financing activity (Broadcom–Anthropic's ~$63bn facility, Meta's up-to-$95bn raise, SpaceX's ~$40bn debt deal) shows how much capital markets are now committing to AI infrastructure; continued investor appetite for this financing remains a key support for the AI trade, and any sign of fatigue would be a risk to watch.
- Boeing's double-digit decline is a reminder that company-specific execution issues can still move markets materially even amid a broad risk-on tape, and is worth monitoring for read-through to the aerospace supply chain.
