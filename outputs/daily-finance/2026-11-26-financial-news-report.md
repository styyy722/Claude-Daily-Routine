## Key Financial News
- US markets were fully closed Thursday 26 November for Thanksgiving (NYSE, Nasdaq and the bond market all shut); the last confirmed session was Wednesday's wild reversal, where the Dow clawed back from an intraday drop of more than 1,100 points to close +0.33% at 50,182.95, while the S&P 500 (-0.25% to 7,182.78) and Nasdaq Composite (-0.62% to 23,585.80) still finished lower on continued hot-inflation, tariff and chip-selling pressure.
- Oil surged overnight into the Thursday Asian session after reports that Saudi forces intercepted drones aimed at the Ras Tanura refinery complex, with WTI up 2.35% to roughly $83.95/bbl and Brent up 3.35% to $94.46/bbl, lifting ASX-listed energy producers Woodside Energy and Santos.
- The ASX 200 (confirmed close) jumped 0.82% (+71.4 points) to 8,763.8, rebounding from Wednesday's 0.4% fall, as Materials (+3.71%) and Energy (+2.67%) led a broad recovery in the miners and gold names that had been hit hardest earlier in the week; the All Ordinaries added 0.82% to 8,866.1.
- Gold miners rallied sharply (All Ordinaries Gold sub-index +5.30%) as spot gold firmed 0.5% to $3,598/oz, bouncing off a one-year low hit earlier in the week, benefiting names such as Newmont and Northern Star.
- AUD/USD consolidated above 0.6600 (last ~0.6635, +0.09%) near an 11-month low, after this week's hotter-than-expected October Australian CPI print pushed back RBA rate-cut expectations; FXStreet's technicals still flag a bearish broader trend, with the RSI in oversold territory.
- Only 5 of the ASX's 11 sectors advanced on the day; defensives lagged the resources-led rally, with Consumer Staples down 1.36% as money rotated out of defensives and back into the previously-beaten-down resources complex.
- In single-stock news, Morgan Stanley reiterated an overweight rating on Technology One and lifted its price target to $40.00 from $32.00, citing the company's AI-driven product roadmap; Liontown, NRW Holdings, Westgold and Evolution Mining all held AGMs.

## Investment Implications
- The sharp, broad-based rotation back into miners and gold stocks after a week of heavy selling suggests Wednesday's AI-led rout may have been overdone in the resources complex specifically, but follow-through over coming sessions is needed before this reads as a durable turn rather than a short-covering bounce.
- A credible Gulf-supply scare at Ras Tanura pushed oil sharply higher overnight; if tensions persist, higher energy costs could complicate both the Fed's and RBA's already-hawkish inflation-fighting stance.
- With US cash and bond markets shut for Thanksgiving, Thursday's AUD/USD and futures trade should be read cautiously given thin holiday liquidity; Friday's shortened US session and the delayed release of Australia's Q3 private capex data will offer a cleaner read on direction into December.
