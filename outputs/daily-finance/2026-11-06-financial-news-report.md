## Key Financial News
- US equities suffered their sharpest one-day selloff in months on Thursday: the Dow fell 1,833.06 points (-3.44%) to 51,523.06, the S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to 7,353.73, and the Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.52% to 24,315.00 — a decline large enough to push the index into correction territory.
- The ASX 200 opened sharply lower on Friday (intraday low 8,923.30) before clawing back most of the loss to close down just 0.49% at 9,020.8, as Materials (-3%) and Technology (-1.4%) led the declines while Consumer Staples (+1%) and the big banks (NAB +2.7%, most majors higher) provided a defensive offset.
- The AI-capex scare had a clear political trigger: most of the 41 US data-centre ballot measures in Tuesday's midterms went against developers, and Texas's incoming governor pledged a temporary moratorium on new data-centre approvals, raising fears of a slower (not necessarily ended) AI infrastructure build-out; Nvidia fell ~9% and Tesla ~11% in Thursday's US trade.
- The selloff went global: South Korea's Kospi triggered a circuit breaker, Japan's Nikkei fell sharply and Taiwan's market dropped more than 8% intraday; the VIX spiked to ~28.4, its biggest intraday jump in over 18 months.
- Bond and currency markets reflected the risk-off/dovish repricing: the US 10-year Treasury yield eased roughly 5.7 basis points to ~4.835% on Thursday as markets priced out one previously expected Fed hike, while AUD/USD slipped 0.5% to 0.6893 despite the broader flight to havens.
- Commodities were mixed: gold fell 1.0% to $4,045/oz (Goldman Sachs cut its end-2026 gold forecast to $4,150/oz from $4,650), iron ore was flat at $93.12/t, copper fell 1.2% on data-centre demand concerns, and oil diverged by benchmark — Brent rose 1.4% to a confirmed $99.67/bbl close while WTI was reported up roughly 5% to ~$91/bbl intraday, with no confirmed official settlement found.
- In company news: Qantas raised its first-half fuel cost estimate to A$3.75bn (shares +0.1%, ~85% hedged for FY27, flagging fare rises and flight cuts); Nine Entertainment (+11%) and Amcor (+5.1%) were among the day's biggest ASX gainers while News Corp (-9.4%), Develop Global (-10%) and IperionX (-8.5%) led losses; David Jones, the 188-year-old department store chain, entered voluntary administration; and Macquarie is under APRA review over a possible breach of non-operating holding company authorisations.

## Investment Implications
- Thursday's selloff reflects a repricing of AI/data-centre capex expectations rather than a broad economic shock — the scale of the ASX's intraday recovery (from an ~1.1% opening loss to a 0.49% close) suggests investors are treating it as a sector-specific valuation correction so far, not a systemic risk event.
- The combination of a hawkish RBA (25bp hike to 4.85% on Tuesday) and a dovish US repricing (one Fed hike removed from the forward outlook after Thursday's rout) creates a diverging rate-path narrative between Australia and the US that is likely to keep AUD/USD volatile.
- Heavy reliance on AI/data-centre-linked names (Nvidia, chipmakers) for recent index gains means further regulatory or political friction around US data-centre approvals is a key swing factor for global tech sentiment in the near term.
