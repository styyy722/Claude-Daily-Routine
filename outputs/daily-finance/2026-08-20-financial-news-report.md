## Key Financial News

- **Treasury buyback relief evaporates:** The 10-year Treasury yield climbed back above the level it held just before Wednesday's announcement that the Treasury would at least double its buybacks of 10-, 20- and 30-year debt, undercutting the prior session's rally and reviving concerns about elevated borrowing costs.
- **Dow sheds over 700 points:** The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 703.84 points (-1.32%) to 52,759.21, the S&P 500 dropped 0.87% to 7,641.16, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.00% to 26,067.17.
- **Walmart weighs on the Dow:** Walmart shares fell roughly 9%, one of the biggest drags on the index, though the broader decline was driven primarily by macro pressure from rising yields rather than company-specific news.
- **Gold and oil firm despite equity weakness:** Gold futures opened near $4,580/oz, up about 0.8%, continuing their advance on debt-related safe-haven demand, while Brent and WTI crude both rose more than 2% on continued geopolitical supply concerns.
- **ASX 200 outperforms:** The index rose 0.33% to 9,083.80, diverging from the weak US session as local earnings-season winners provided support ahead of Friday's trading.

## Investment Implications

- The reversal of Wednesday's yield relief suggests the Treasury buyback program alone may not be sufficient to durably cap borrowing costs, keeping rate-sensitive equities vulnerable to further volatility.
- Company-specific weakness in defensive names like Walmart, layered on top of a macro-driven sell-off, signals broad-based risk-off positioning rather than a single-sector rotation.
- The ASX 200's resilience relative to US markets highlights how local earnings catalysts can offset global rate-driven headwinds, at least in the near term.
