## Key Financial News
- Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as President, CEO and a board director effective immediately, less than two weeks after his Nov. 12 resignation; board chair Harvey C. Jones said "the Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA." President Trump called the reversal "an absolutely GREAT decision." Nvidia shares jumped roughly 31–35% intraday (reports range $165–$166, adding up to roughly $1 trillion in market value), its biggest one-day move since the stock began trading.
- The rally lifted the broader market off Friday's correction-territory close: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite had their worst week since the COVID-19 crash before Monday's bounce, with Nomura strategist Charlie McElligott describing Monday's move as a "Mechanical Bounce" driven by forced short-covering and options-related hedging rather than a confirmed trend reversal.
- The VIX fell sharply intraday (to roughly 27.5, down about 23%) as volatility eased from Friday's highs, though it remained well above its long-run average.
- The ASX 200 fell for an eighth straight session to a fresh 100-day low (confirmed close ~8,570.6, -0.26%), even as Wall Street futures pointed sharply higher late in the local session; banks and property trusts were the biggest drags (CBA -2.2%, Westpac -1.9%) while miners rebounded on bargain-hunting (BHP +2.2%), Sims +9.6%, and ALS fell more than 12% on the day's worst performers list.
- Fed policy-rate odds for the 9–10 December FOMC meeting were tracked at roughly 91–92% probability of a further 25bp hike across Polymarket and bond-market pricing, underscoring that the rally has not shifted the hawkish rate outlook.
- Anthropic (ANT) shares fell about 1.7% intraday, trading below $88, extending a volatile first week since its record-breaking IPO debut.
- Gold rose about 2.2% to roughly $3,609/oz (spot, Australian-afternoon reading) and oil edged modestly higher (WTI ~$83.55/bbl, Brent ~$92.65/bbl) after an earlier intraday dip, while iron ore slipped slightly on the SGX December benchmark (~$88.25/t, -0.25%).

## Investment Implications
- Monday's rebound was concentrated in a single company-specific catalyst (Huang's reinstatement) rather than a broad fundamental shift, and strategists quoted in coverage (e.g., Nomura's McElligott) caution that mechanical, hedging-driven bounces of this kind are not reliable signals of a sustained recovery after a correction.
- The ASX 200's eighth consecutive losing session, even as US futures surged, suggests local market sentiment (banks, property trusts) has decoupled somewhat from the AI-led rebound story and may lag any genuine stabilisation in US tech/semiconductor names.
- A near-92% probability of a further Fed rate hike in December, unchanged by Monday's rally, means the hawkish policy backdrop remains a headwind for risk assets even during sharp relief rallies.
