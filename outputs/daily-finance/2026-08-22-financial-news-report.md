## Key Financial News
- **US equities rebound**: The S&P 500 (+0.43% to 7,674.37), Dow (+0.98%/+517.80 pts to 53,277.01) and Nasdaq Composite (+0.43% to 26,180.45) all rose Friday, clawing back part of Thursday's rout (Dow -703.84 pts/-1.3%, S&P -0.9%, Nasdaq -1.0%), when Walmart shares fell 9.2% on its results and a spike in bond yields spooked investors. The VIX had jumped 7.5% to 16.01 on Thursday before easing Friday.
- **Bond yields**: The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.74%, testing 20-month highs, as heavy debt issuance from AI-related capex and higher federal deficit spending kept upward pressure on the long end of the curve — even after the Treasury's announcement of larger buybacks briefly pulled yields lower earlier in the week.
- **Gold rally**: Spot gold climbed toward $4,600–4,607/oz — its highest since mid-May — supported by a softer US dollar and fresh concerns about US fiscal sustainability following the Treasury's unexpected increase in planned long-dated debt purchases. Gold notched a third consecutive weekly gain of roughly 5%.
- **Oil holds steady**: WTI crude eased slightly to $86.64/bbl (-0.22%) while Brent held near $94, with prices still underpinned by US-Iran tensions and threats of tighter sanctions that raise the risk of supply disruption via the Strait of Hormuz.
- **Australian market**: The ASX 200 closed down 0.27% (-24 pts) to 9,058.90 on Friday. Zip Co was the index's worst performer (-14.6%) and Telix Pharmaceuticals also fell sharply (-9.7%). Iron ore edged up 0.09% to about $95.4/tonne.
- **RBA outlook**: NAB forecasts the RBA's next move to be a rate cut, with a gradual normalisation beginning mid-2027; the central bank is otherwise expected to remain on hold through the rest of 2026.
- **Corporate news**: James Hardie is reportedly planning a billion-dollar exit from its European operations; Origin Energy issued a customer update on a data security incident; Subaru is being taken to Federal Court by the ACCC over alleged failure to give independent mechanics equal access to repair information.
- **US macro data**: Initial jobless claims fell 6,000 to 206,000 (week ended Aug 15) while continuing claims rose 18,000 to 1.799 million; the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index surged to 47.4, well above the 25 consensus; the Leading Economic Indicators index rose 0.2% month-over-month in July.

## Investment Implications
- Friday's rebound suggests some stabilization after Thursday's yield-driven sell-off, but persistently elevated long-end Treasury yields (near 20-month highs) remain a headwind for equity valuations, particularly rate-sensitive and high-multiple growth names.
- Gold's breakout to three-month highs reflects growing investor demand for safe havens amid US fiscal-sustainability concerns and dollar weakness — a trend worth monitoring as a signal of broader risk sentiment.
- A softer AUD/USD-supportive backdrop alongside a still-cautious RBA outlook (rate cuts not expected before mid-2027) points to a mixed setup for Australian equities, with idiosyncratic stock-specific moves (Zip Co, Telix) outweighing broad index direction this week.
