## Key Financial News
- Nvidia reported fiscal Q3 2027 (quarter ended October 25, 2026) revenue of $115.8 billion — more than 100% above the $57.0 billion reported a year earlier and above the company's own $108.0 billion (±2%) guidance and the roughly $108.7 billion consensus — with GAAP gross margin of 74.8%, operating income of $77.5 billion, net income of $65.3 billion and diluted EPS of $2.70, versus a consensus estimate of about $2.47.
- Despite the beat, Nvidia shares traded roughly 2% lower at midday Wednesday, which commentators described as a "muted reaction," arguing much of the expected AI-driven growth is already priced into the stock after its run-up.
- This was Nvidia's first quarterly report since turmoil around CEO Jensen Huang's leadership status; interim CEO Colette Kress hosted Tuesday evening's earnings call, with the leadership situation still not fully clarified in sources checked.
- US equities were volatile on Wednesday: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell as much as roughly 1.2–1.7% intraday on continued weakness, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite swung between small gains and losses as investors weighed the Nvidia reaction against broader AI-sector sentiment.
- The US Dollar Index extended a stretch of strength, with commentary pointing to a multi-month (around 19-month) high, continuing a move that began earlier in the week on hawkish Federal Reserve commentary pointing to a possible December rate rise.
- The ASX 200 closed down 1.8% on Tuesday at 8,883.20 and fell a further 0.69% (-61.4 points) on Wednesday to 8,821.8, closing near the session's intraday low as gold miners and iron-ore names led losses (Champion Iron -4.1%, Mineral Resources -3.4%).
- Separately, Anthropic was reported to be preparing to price its IPO this week, a closely watched event for AI-sector sentiment alongside Nvidia's results.

## Investment Implications
- Nvidia's "beat but sell the news" reaction suggests the market is becoming more discerning about AI-related earnings, rewarding confirmation of growth less than it has in prior quarters — a dynamic worth watching across the broader semiconductor and AI-infrastructure trade.
- A firmer US dollar alongside hawkish Fed commentary is a headwind for AUD/USD and commodity-linked currencies generally, though a confirmed November 18 AUD/USD level was not available in sources checked.
- Back-to-back declines in the ASX 200, concentrated in miners and gold stocks, point to a domestically-led risk-off tone in Australian equities that is only partly explained by the overnight Wall Street picture, given the mixed/incomplete nature of Wednesday's US intraday data.
