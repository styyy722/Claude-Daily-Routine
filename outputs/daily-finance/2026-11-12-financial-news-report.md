## Key Financial News
- Wall Street rallied Wednesday for a second straight session (S&P 500 +1.3% to 7,619, Dow +0.5% to 52,486, Nasdaq Composite +1.6% to 25,466), even as Reuters and other outlets reported Nvidia's board had scheduled a meeting to decide CEO Jensen Huang's future following allegations of extramarital affairs, including a reported relationship with an Nvidia VP that US intelligence had scrutinised for possible ties to a suspected Chinese-intelligence-linked individual; the company declined to comment on the board meeting.
- Nvidia shares, which peaked above US$237 in early October, had fallen to around US$158.78 (roughly -33%, into bear-market territory) and dropped more than 8% further in after-hours trade on the governance news. Analyst Dan Ives put Nvidia's value at roughly US$300/share with Huang remaining CEO versus US$225 without him; Morningstar's Brian Colello flagged VP Ian Buck as a possible internal successor.
- The ASX 200 closed down 6.4 points (-0.07%) at 9,146.8, snapping a three-day winning streak, after an early slide to an intraday low of 9,110.9 (-0.5%) on weak US futures and the Nvidia headlines was largely recovered through the afternoon. Materials was the weakest of 11 sectors (-2.08%) as gold and lithium miners sold off — Vulcan Energy Resources -8.91%, Deep Yellow -8.52%, Rio Tinto -2.14%, Fortescue -2.40%, BHP -1.29% — while financials (+1.10%) cushioned the index on a strong rebound in Commonwealth Bank (+2.89%) and Westpac (+1.93%, a three-month high). Market breadth was negative (448 advancers vs 633 decliners).
- Xero shares jumped as much as 10.4% intraday before closing +6.08% after raising its revenue outlook for the year to March 2027 and announcing an on-market share buy-back of up to A$1 billion. Domino's Pizza Enterprises surged 12.93% following a trading update, while Aristocrat Leisure fell roughly 1.3–4.8% after full-year results were accompanied by a chief-executive transition announcement.
- US and Australian bond yields remained elevated (US 10-year ~4.99%, Australian 10-year ~5.04%); Capital.com's Kyle Rodda said traders are pricing in a further Fed rate move at the December meeting. ANZ CEO Nuno Matos described record-high interest rates coinciding with record-high equity markets as a "dangerous situation," while APRA noted long-term government bond yields across advanced economies are near their highest level in at least 15 years.
- AUD/USD eased modestly to around 68.31 US cents (-0.2%). Gold slipped 0.4% to roughly US$3,971/oz as the All Ordinaries Gold index fell 3.59% alongside the broader miner sell-off. Brent crude eased 1.2% to US$91.67/bbl; iron ore fell 0.6% to roughly US$92.00/tonne.
- The CEOs of National Australia Bank and Commonwealth Bank are scheduled to appear before a House of Representatives economics committee hearing on Friday.

## Investment Implications
- The Nvidia governance story has become a standalone source of market risk for AI/semiconductor sentiment, distinct from macro data; the board's eventual decision on Huang's status — and any detail on the underlying allegations — could move the stock and broader tech-led indices sharply in either direction.
- The ASX's ability to finish near-flat despite a sharp intraday materials/gold sell-off shows domestic financials strength is currently absorbing commodity-sector volatility, but a sustained slide in gold and lithium miners would weigh further on the index if it persists.
- Elevated bond yields alongside record or near-record equity levels — explicitly flagged as a risk by ANZ's CEO — point to a valuation and duration sensitivity that is worth monitoring as central bank rate-path commentary continues.
