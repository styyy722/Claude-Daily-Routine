## Key Financial News
- **Wall Street hits fresh records on chip strength**: The S&P 500 (+0.58% to 7,818.93) and Nasdaq Composite (+0.45% to 27,599.79) both closed at record highs as Nvidia and AMD reached all-time-high share prices; AMD CEO Lisa Su said "demand for compute continues to outrun supply," underscoring continued AI-infrastructure demand. Nvidia's market cap approached $6 trillion.
- **Treasury yields ease from multi-decade high**: The 10-year yield fell to 5.27% (-4bps), providing relief to growth equities after touching a 24-year high on Monday.
- **Semiconductor and energy-deal headlines add to momentum**: Marvell Technology jumped 8% after raising its fiscal 2028 revenue guidance to $20 billion; Constellation Energy surged 13% on a $4.3 billion nuclear-power supply deal with Google.
- **Market breadth remains narrow**: Despite the record index closes, only about 25% of S&P 500 constituents traded above their 50-day moving average, highlighting continued concentration in mega-cap tech names.
- **ASX 200 sets a fresh record on broad gains**: Australia's benchmark rose 0.57% to 8,735.70, a new closing high, with gains across 10 of 11 sectors (only consumer staples slipped); BHP alone contributed roughly half of the day's points gain, with utilities and energy also leading.
- **RBA rate-cut odds priced out**: Stronger-than-expected household spending data reinforced the RBA's concern about sticky inflation, and bond markets have now priced out any rate cut this cycle; the next RBA decision is 2–3 November.
- **Oil eases, gold and iron ore mixed**: WTI crude traded around $89.92/bbl (+0.55% vs Monday's close) as Middle Eastern supply concerns eased and G7 members signalled potential strategic-reserve releases; gold futures rose 0.85% to $4,191.50/oz while iron ore slipped 0.30% to ~$91.18/T.

## Investment Implications
- The combination of record index closes alongside narrow breadth (only ~25% of S&P 500 names above their 50-day average) suggests continued concentration risk for investors with heavy mega-cap tech exposure, even as headline indices advance.
- Easing Treasury yields and the AI-infrastructure capex narrative (Marvell, Nvidia, AMD) remain the dominant tailwinds for US equities; any reversal in yields or a slowdown in AI-related capital spending commentary would be a key risk to watch.
- With RBA rate-cut odds now priced out and the next decision not until 2–3 November, near-term ASX 200 and AUD/USD direction is likely to be driven more by commodity-linked large caps (e.g., BHP) and US data flow than by domestic policy news in the immediate term.
