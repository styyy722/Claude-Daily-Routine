- US equities rebounded Friday (S&P 500 +0.43%, Dow +0.98%, Nasdaq +0.43%) after a flash PMI reading showed US business activity growing at its fastest pace in more than four years, but all three indices still posted weekly losses as Treasury yields stayed volatile.

## Key Financial News
- **US equities**: The S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq all rose Friday, but each still closed the week lower — the S&P 500 down 1.4%, the Dow down 0.8%, and the Nasdaq down 2% over five sessions — as bond-market volatility weighed on sentiment earlier in the week.
- **US business activity**: S&P Global's flash Composite PMI jumped to 56.0 in August from 54.5 in July, the strongest reading since April 2022. Services led at 56.8 (a 20-month high) while manufacturing eased to 53.2. Hiring accelerated to its fastest pace since January 2025, and Q3 growth is now tracking toward roughly 3.0% annualized, up from 1.5% in Q2.
- **Bond yields**: The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.73% and the 30-year to 5.27%. Treasury Secretary Bessent's expanded bond buyback program gave only temporary relief, with yields "snapping back" as markets questioned its effectiveness; over the weekend Trump suggested further market "intervention" was possible.
- **Middle East / oil**: WTI crude rose 2.7% to $88.15/bbl and Brent gained 2% to $93.45/bbl on Friday amid Hormuz-related supply concerns. Over the weekend, US claims that dozens of tankers safely cleared the Strait were disputed by tracking data and Iran itself; Iran's national security secretariat warned it would strike US economic interests in the region and block Gulf oil exports if sanctions pressure continues. Bessent is set to hold a press conference Monday 2pm ET on the next phase of sanctions.
- **US-Canada trade**: Talks between Washington and Ottawa collapsed over the weekend; the US imposed a 50% tariff on Canadian goods, and Canada said it will match US tariffs dollar-for-dollar starting September 8 — a marked escalation in North American trade tensions heading into the new week.
- **Australian market**: The ASX 200 slipped 0.27% to 9,058.90 on Friday (-0.62% for the week). Westpac now expects the RBA to hold rates through the rest of 2026, with the next move a cut beginning mid-2027; Australian unemployment ticked up to 4.5% as employers cut hours rather than jobs, while the estimated probability of a 2026 technical recession fell to 3.5% from 6.0%.
- **Crypto & other**: Bitcoin surged to $77,569 (+0.94% Friday), its best week in two years at +22%. BJ's Wholesale Club beat earnings estimates with 11.9% same-store sales growth. Public opposition to AI data-center buildout was reported rising sharply (from ~40% to ~75%), a watch point for the broader AI capex/data-center theme.

## Investment Implications
- Friday's strong PMI print signals resilient US growth momentum, but persistently elevated long-end Treasury yields (10-year near 4.73%, 30-year above 5.25%) remain a headwind for equity valuations and rate-sensitive sectors.
- Escalating Iran-related Gulf tensions and a fresh US-Canada tariff war are two distinct geopolitical/trade risks that could pressure energy prices and supply chains simultaneously — a combination worth monitoring for inflation and margin impacts.
- Australia's softening labour market alongside a lower recession probability suggests a "muddle-through" backdrop for AUD-sensitive and rate-sensitive Australian assets, with the RBA now expected to stay on hold well into 2027.
