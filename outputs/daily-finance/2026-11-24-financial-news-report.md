## Key Financial News
- Nvidia shares fell roughly 7.5% a day after jumping 31.5% on Monday's surprise reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO — the stock's biggest one-day gain in more than two decades — as investors concluded the move hadn't resolved underlying doubts about whether AI/data-center spending can be justified by returns, and amid renewed regulatory concern following Democrats' midterm wins.
- Anthropic (ANT) shares fell about 9.3%, dropping below the $83 price at which it priced its IPO just over a week ago; the stock had briefly traded as high as $115 on its debut day.
- Other AI-linked names also fell sharply: CoreWeave sank 13% and GE Vernova dropped 5.6%, while the Nasdaq Composite's decline (~2%) outpaced a roughly 0.9% drop in the S&P 500.
- The Dow Jones rose about 0.55%, as strength in non-AI-exposed stocks offset the tech-led declines — a divergence underscoring how concentrated recent volatility has been in AI/data-center names. Asian markets outside the AI-pullback story were firm, with South Korea's Kospi up 6.3% and Japan's Nikkei 225 up 3.9%.
- The ASX 200 (confirmed close) jumped 1.82% to 8,726.40, a sharp rebound after a recent multi-session losing streak.
- Gold fell roughly 2.6% to about $3,556/oz as the US dollar held near a 20-month high and traders weighed the prospect of higher-for-longer US interest rates.
- Oil rose about 2% (WTI ~$81.77/bbl, Brent ~$88.49/bbl), with Brent having swung between roughly $72 and $114 since February amid continued Middle East-linked supply uncertainty; the 10-year US Treasury yield was little changed at around 4.91%.

## Investment Implications
- Monday's AI-sector rally proved short-lived, suggesting investor concerns about data-center spending returns and high interest rates remain unresolved rather than settled by a single management change.
- The sharp divergence between the Dow (up) and Nasdaq (down) points to continued rotation out of richly valued AI/tech megacaps into broader, less AI-exposed names.
- Firmer oil prices alongside a stronger US dollar are a headwind for gold and could add to inflation-related uncertainty feeding into central bank rate decisions.
