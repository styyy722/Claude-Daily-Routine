# Daily Financial News Report – 2026-10-14

## Key Financial News
- **Hot September CPI rattles markets**: Headline CPI rose 0.6% month-over-month and 3.7% year-over-year (vs. 3.6% expected); core CPI rose 0.3% m/m and 2.6% y/y (vs. 2.5% expected), with higher gas and energy prices a key driver.
- **Wall Street sells off on rate-hike fears**: The S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.88% to 26,692.87; the Dow was comparatively resilient, down only 0.09% (-48.62pts) to 51,621.48.
- **Small caps and tech hit hardest**: The Russell 2000 fell 1.93% to 2,729.53, and losses were concentrated in tech and semiconductor stocks, with some reports tying the drop to a continued sell-off in AI-related names.
- **Defensive sectors outperform**: Healthcare and consumer staples were among the S&P 500's relative winners as investors rotated away from rate-sensitive growth names.
- **Gold slides on reinvigorated Fed-hike bets**: Spot gold fell roughly 1.2% to around $4,110/oz as the hotter CPI print pushed back against hopes for near-term rate cuts.
- **ASX 200 weighed down by banks and gold miners**: The index fell 0.64% (-56pts) to 8,727.9, with major bank stocks including CBA weighing most heavily, while the All Ordinaries Gold Index dropped about 1.93% as the worst-performing sector.
- **Oil holds in the mid-$90s**: WTI crude traded around $95/bbl, with reports varying between roughly $94.20 and $95.80, keeping energy costs — and their inflationary pass-through — in focus.

## Investment Implications
- A hotter-than-expected CPI print reduces the likelihood of near-term Fed rate cuts and raises the odds markets price in further tightening, a headwind for rate-sensitive growth, tech, and small-cap stocks.
- The simultaneous decline in both bank stocks and gold miners on the ASX suggests a broad repricing of rate expectations rather than a classic flight-to-safety trade, with few sectors fully insulated from the move.
- Persistently elevated energy prices (oil in the mid-$90s) are feeding directly into inflation readings, creating a feedback loop that could keep yields and rate expectations elevated for longer.
