## Key Financial News
- **Weekend escalation in the Strait of Hormuz conflict**: US forces struck an Iranian crude oil tanker (M/T "Kylo", also called "Noxen") in the Gulf of Oman on 5 September in retaliation for attacks on US-linked vessels; CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper warned "if you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours." ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-centcom-targeted-iranian-forces-posed/?id=136080582))
- **US diesel prices hit four-year high**: The tanker strike coincided with America's diesel bill breaking a four-year record, underscoring the pass-through of Strait of Hormuz disruption into fuel costs. ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/usa-canada-intelligence-brief-saturday-september-5-2026/))
- **Fed repricing continues into the weekend**: The 2-year Treasury yield rose to ~4.40% as markets further priced in the chance of a Fed rate hike; Fed Governor Michael Barr said he would back a rate hike if inflation does not moderate soon enough, adding to hawkish signals following Friday's strong payrolls print. ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/usa-canada-intelligence-brief-saturday-september-5-2026/))
- **Treasury pushes to isolate Iran financially**: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is reportedly pushing for measures to financially isolate Iran, while President Trump downplayed the conflict's economic significance for the US, calling it "small potatoes." ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/usa-canada-intelligence-brief-saturday-september-5-2026/))
- **Iron ore ticks higher**: The 62% Fe benchmark for China delivery settled at $99.57/tonne on 5 September, up 0.15%, holding within its recent $93-$100 range. ([Rio Times – Iron Ore](https://www.riotimesonline.com/iron-ore-markets-latam-saturday-september-5-2026/))
- **Gold little changed over the weekend**: Spot gold traded around $4,476.60/oz, broadly steady from Friday's close near $4,478/oz after Friday's post-payrolls pullback. ([Convex](https://convextrade.com/metrics/gold))
- **US Labor Day ahead**: US equity and bond markets are closed Monday 7 September for the Labor Day holiday; the next full US trading session is Tuesday 8 September, meaning markets will digest the weekend's Iran developments and diesel/Fed headlines over an extended gap.

## Investment Implications
- The weekend tanker strike raises the risk of further Strait of Hormuz escalation heading into next week, which could keep energy and shipping-cost pressure elevated regardless of Monday's holiday lull.
- Rising short-end yields (2-year near 4.40%) alongside hawkish Fed commentary (Governor Barr) suggest markets are continuing to build in September rate-hike risk ahead of the 15-16 FOMC meeting; this dynamic bears watching into Tuesday's reopening.
- The extended weekend gap (no US trading Monday) means any further Iran-related developments over the holiday could produce an outsized reaction when markets reopen Tuesday.
