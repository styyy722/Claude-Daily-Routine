## Key Financial News
- US tech stocks led losses again Monday (Nasdaq Composite -1.07%) as AI-trade and chipmaker concerns persisted, while the Dow Jones gained 0.41% on rotation into non-tech sectors; the S&P 500 fell 0.51%.
- The ASX 200 closed down ~0.6% to ~8,857 in Monday's Sydney session, pulling back from Friday's 8,908 close.
- Oil (WTI) was the day's biggest single mover, plunging roughly 8% (-$8.39 to $95.73/bbl) during US trading hours on reports of diplomatic progress between the US and Iran toward resolving Middle East tensions; the milder ~-2% move seen earlier in the Sydney session understates the scale of the later US-hours drop.
- Gold rebounded 1.49% (+$58.20) to $3,957.10/oz, bouncing off Friday's near-one-year low.
- Westpac reported FY26 net profit of A$6.8 billion (cash profit A$7.0 billion), modestly above the A$6.96 billion consensus; shares rose 1.0%, outperforming other major banks despite a softer net interest margin (1.87% vs. 1.89% expected).
- Australian housing data showed national home values fell 1.2% in October — a seventh consecutive monthly decline — with AMP now forecasting a 15% peak-to-trough fall; the Australian manufacturing PMI also slipped into contraction at 49.6.
- Apple reported fiscal Q4 results after Monday's US close (consensus estimates were ~$2 EPS on ~$113.46 billion revenue); actual figures were not yet available from the sources checked and are not reported here.
- Fallout from Friday's disclosed Binance security breach continued, with an estimated US$3.6–4.4 billion in customer withdrawals reported.

## Investment Implications
- The divergence between a weaker Nasdaq and a firmer Dow suggests continued rotation out of high-multiple AI/tech names into more defensive or value-oriented sectors; investors should watch whether this broadens or reverses once Apple's results are digested.
- Oil's sharp US-session drop on US–Iran diplomacy headlines is a reminder that geopolitical risk premia can unwind quickly; energy-sector positioning should account for continued headline sensitivity.
- With the RBA widely expected to hike Tuesday, AUD and rate-sensitive Australian sectors (banks, housing-linked names, retailers) are likely to see elevated volatility around the 2:30pm AEDT decision.
